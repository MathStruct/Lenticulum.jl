#overview

> Section Q of the [[Feature Backlog]]: bringing the whole repository to the engineering standard of the
> SciML and JuliaHub packages (Krylov.jl, LinearSolve.jl, NonlinearSolve.jl, DifferentiationInterface.jl).
> That standard covers types usable for dispatch, independence from the AD backend and from the array
> type, parallel execution, and profiling and benchmarks kept in CI. The section also covers
> compiling the factor graph ahead of time, with Sophia as the compiler backend. It was asked for on
> 2026-11-24. Open rows only; a row that is done moves to [[Backlog Done 2026-11]] (or that month's note)
> with its id.

> Sources: the maintainer's request of 2026-11-24 ("as good or better quality as the SciML/JuliaHub
> organization has"; consolidation is fine where there is a reason for it); [[Shared Solver Layer]]
> "Why not Krylov.jl" (P40); Sophia's `vault/Storage and Materialization/AD as a Materialization Case.md`
> (E-AD-1, E-7) and its backlog row B-093; code: `tools/type_audit.jl`, `bench/krylov_allocations.jl`,
> `bench/structure_compile.jl`

## The baseline (2026-11-24, `8544754d`)

What the rows start from, measured on `dev-danielb`. The commands are given so the baseline can be
measured again.

| criterion | where it stands | how it was measured |
|---|---|---|
| types for dispatch | 147 of 223 `struct` definitions in `lib/*/src` are parametric. Fields with a non-concrete type, or a container of an abstract element type: Mycelium 30 (`FactorGraph.plates::Vector{Any}`, `emit_plans::Dict{…,Any}`, `energy_rows::RefValue{Any}`, `EdgeMessages.msgs`/`blocks::Vector{Any}`, `MessageStore.pins::Dict{Int,Any}`, `ClusterFactor.factors`, `VectorBlock.beliefs`, `PlateSchedule.plans`, `SampleBuffer.entries`, `_ResidualMemory`, `_CoordinateStore`, …), Lenticulum 3, LenticulumCore 2, MyceliumLearning 2, ImplicitLayers 1, the other four 0. Seven closures in Mycelium and two in MyceliumLearning capture a variable as `Any`. `FactorGraph.factors::Vector{FactorNode}` holds a `UnionAll`, so each factor's message call is a dynamic dispatch (I2). The algorithm types of ImplicitSolvers (`GMRES`, `MINRES`) carry no type parameters and no workspace (P40) | `julia --project=lib/<Pkg>.jl tools/type_audit.jl <Pkg>`; `grep -rhoE '^\s*(mutable )?struct [A-Za-z0-9_]+\{?' lib/*/src` |
| AD backend | AD enters through weak dependencies and extensions (ChainRulesCore, ForwardDiff, DifferentiationInterface, EnzymeCore and Mooncake in ImplicitLayers); no package depends hard on an AD package. The tests run on a subset of backends. Mycelium and Lenticulum: Zygote and ForwardDiff only. LenticulumCore: Zygote, ForwardDiff and 7 Enzyme. MyceliumLearning: 2 Enzyme and 2 Mooncake beside Zygote and ForwardDiff. ImplicitLayers and VariationalDiffusion: all four. ImplicitSolvers: ForwardDiff only. Parts of Mycelium are written functional "for Zygote" (`free_energy.jl` 139, 161), which works against in-place kernels | `grep -rhoE 'Auto(Zygote\|ForwardDiff\|Enzyme\|Mooncake)\|Enzyme\.\|Mooncake' lib/<Pkg>/test` |
| array type | JLArrays tests exist in ImplicitSolvers, Mycelium, MyceliumLearning, ImplicitLayers and VariationalDiffusion. Mycelium's, MyceliumLearning's and ImplicitLayers' run only with `LENTICULUM_DEVICE_TESTS=true` (weekly in the coverage job). None exist in Lenticulum (the factors) or LenticulumCore. No hardware GPU job exists; VariationalDiffusion's `test/reactant/` runs Reactant on the CPU, outside the suite. Literal allocations in `lib/*/src` (`zeros`/`ones`/`fill`): Lenticulum 108, Mycelium 45, VariationalDiffusion 45, AlgebraicFactors 26. Mentions of `Float64`: Lenticulum 46, Mycelium 37, AlgebraicFactors 27. Neither count separates the allocations with an element type from the input from those without. No package uses Adapt.jl | `grep -rlE JLArray lib/*/test`; `grep -rcE '\bzeros\(\|\bones\(\|\bfill\(' lib/<Pkg>/src` |
| parallel | Nothing in `lib/*/src` uses `Threads`, `@spawn`, `@threads`, OhMyThreads, Polyester or KernelAbstractions. CI sets `JULIA_NUM_THREADS=auto` only in two weekly jobs (`Weekly.yml`), the coverage job among them; the suites on push run on the default thread count | `grep -rnE 'Threads\.\|@threads\|@spawn' lib/*/src` |
| profiling and benchmarks | 26 scripts in `bench/` besides the runner, recorded in notes, all run small by `bench/smoke.jl` (weekly). None uses BenchmarkTools, nothing compares a pull request with `master`, and T2 (regression tracking) is open. No JET tests (T3 open). Three test files check allocations (ImplicitLayers `solver_numerics.jl`, Lenticulum `coupled_keys.jl`, `zygote_keys.jl`). No PrecompileTools workload. First gradient on a new structure, from `bench/structure_compile.md` (Zygote through `infer!`, 2026-11-22): 2.04 s at 31 factors and 4.53 s at 121, against 0.011 and 0.039 s warm, so the first call costs 120–200 warm calls | `grep -rlE '@allocated\|@ballocated\|JET' lib/*/test` |

So the types of the solver and factor layers are mostly sound. The gaps are in the graph containers,
the AD coverage of the inference core, devices for the factors, threading (none), and benchmarking
as a gate rather than a record.

| id | feature | status | needs | priority | source | why |

| Q1 | **The standard, written down.** One design note, `vault/Design/Engineering Standard.md`, says per package and per layer what applies. Kernels (messages, energies, solvers, plate blocks) are held to all five criteria. Graph construction and structure edits may stay dynamic behind a function barrier. The note also says which CI job enforces each criterion. It includes a dependency policy: use the SciML/JuliaHub package where it meets the bar, and write our own only with a recorded reason and a measurement (the lesson of P40). It decides the style conflict between "functional for Zygote" and in-place kernels, for example in-place primal kernels with rules for the adjoint, as LinearSolve and NonlinearSolve do | proposed | — | high | the request of 2026-11-24 | without one bar, each row below invents its own; P14 dropped Krylov.jl with no recorded reason |
| Q2 | **Typed graph containers.** Each of the 30 Mycelium fields in the baseline (and the 8 elsewhere) gets a concrete type or a type parameter. Where it stays erased on purpose (a cache keyed at run time), the reason, the function barrier and its measured cost go into the source note. The 9 closures that capture `Any` are fixed. Done when `tools/type_audit.jl` lists only the documented exceptions, and it runs in CI | proposed | — | medium | baseline above | `Vector{Any}` in the message store and the plates puts dynamic dispatch and boxing on the hot path, and blocks compiling a sweep (Q16) |
| Q3 | **Type stability as a gate.** JET `@test_opt` (`report_opt`) on every package's public entry points and inner kernels, called with concrete types (T3 is the first part: the Gaussian and constraint messages and the plate kernels). Exceptions go into a JET test file with their reason, as they do in `test/aqua.jl` now | proposed | T3 | medium | baseline above | no package has a JET test; type instability in a kernel goes unnoticed until a benchmark |
| Q4 | **Element type from the input.** Every allocation in a compute path takes its element type and array type from its inputs (`similar`, `zero.(x)`, `eltype`), not a literal `Float64` or `zeros(n)`. Every factor's messages and energy are tested in `Float32`, `Float64`, `BigFloat` and `ForwardDiff.Dual` against the `Float64` result. Done when the tests pass for every factor type in Lenticulum, LenticulumCore and AlgebraicFactors | proposed | — | medium | baseline above (Lenticulum 108 literal allocations, 46 `Float64`) | Float32 on GPUs, BigFloat for reference runs, and duals for forward mode all need it |
| Q5 | **An AD backend matrix.** Each differentiable entry point is tested against finite differences with Zygote, ForwardDiff, Enzyme, Mooncake and, where it applies, Reactant. The entry points are factor energies, messages, `free_energy`, `infer!`, the graph adjoint, the EM statistics and ImplicitSolvers' solves. The tests use DifferentiationInterfaceTest scenarios where they fit, and a backend that fails is a `@test_broken` with its reason. Public functions take an `ADTypes` backend, never a package name | proposed | — | high | baseline above (Mycelium and Lenticulum: Zygote and ForwardDiff only) | "agnostic to the AD backend" is untested for the inference core; Enzyme is the backend that works with in-place kernels (Q1) and with Reactant (Q16) |
| Q6 | **Native rules where an adjoint is implicit.** Enzyme rules (EnzymeRules) and Mooncake rules (or `@from_rrule`) for the implicit adjoints. These are ImplicitSolvers' `linsolve`, `solve_root` and `solve_columns`, the graph adjoint, `infer!`'s replay and the implicit-diffusion query. Without the rules, Enzyme and Mooncake differentiate through the iterations. ImplicitLayers has EnzymeCore and Mooncake extensions; the other packages have only ChainRulesCore. Done when Q5's matrix passes for these entry points without differentiating the solver loop (checked by counting operator calls) | proposed | Q5 | medium | baseline above | differentiating through a Krylov or Newton loop is slow, memory-hungry and inexact; LinearSolve and NonlinearSolve ship adjoint rules for every backend |
| Q7 | **Device-generic factors.** Every factor type's messages, energy and M-step are tested on JLArrays with `allowscalar(false)`, for Lenticulum and LenticulumCore (none today), in the default suite and not behind `LENTICULUM_DEVICE_TESTS`. If that is too slow, a fast subset runs in the default suite. Structs that hold arrays get `Adapt.adapt_structure`, so a whole graph moves with `adapt(CuArray, g)`. The array-type matrix: `Array`, views, `SArray` where dimensions are small, `JLArray` | proposed | Q4 | high | baseline above | the factors are the one layer with no device test; Reactant (Q16) forbids scalar indexing just as JLArrays does |
| Q8 | **A hardware GPU job.** A CI job runs the device tests on CUDA at least weekly; JuliaGPU's Buildkite is offered to open-source Julia packages, otherwise a self-hosted runner. It includes the device benchmarks (kernel time, transfers, synchronisations). The open question of [[Shared Solver Layer]] (no device benchmark) is answered here | proposed | Q7 | medium | baseline above; Status "Not run: device tests with a GPU" in every recent row | JLArrays catches scalar indexing, not performance or kernel failures; no code has ever run on a GPU in CI |
| Q9 | **Thread safety, audited.** Every mutable shared state is listed with what guards it: `FactorGraph`'s caches under `polarity_lock`, the `MessageStore`, `SampleBuffer`, the residual queue and RNG streams. Each gets a test that runs it from several tasks. Includes the Julia 1.12 interactive-pool pitfall (`bench/jit_latency.jl`) | proposed | — | medium | baseline above | Q10 needs it; caches written during a sweep are the likely races |
| Q10 | **Parallel execution.** Thread-parallel kernels where the work is independent: the factor→variable messages of a flooding sweep, the blocks of a plate, the EM statistics over data, the scoring of structure candidates (S7), and independent restarts and chains. The reduction order is fixed, so results do not depend on the thread count (bitwise, or within a stated tolerance). A scaling benchmark at 1, 2, 4 and 8 threads, with its note. OhMyThreads.jl or `Threads.@spawn` over chunks; KernelAbstractions where the same kernel should also run on a device | proposed | Q9 | medium | baseline above (no threading in `lib/*/src`) | every workload today uses one core; a flooding sweep is embarrassingly parallel |
| Q11 | **The SciML problem/cache interface for solves.** `init(problem, alg)` gives a cache holding typed workspaces, `solve!(cache)` reuses it, and `solve` is the convenience form. Applies to `linsolve`, `solve_root`, `solve_columns`, `infer!` (a message store and plan reused across calls) and the EM step. Operators are applied with `mul!` and preconditioners with `ldiv!`. P40 decides whether the Krylov part is Krylov.jl's workspaces | proposed | Q1 | high | [[Shared Solver Layer]] "Why not Krylov.jl"; `bench/krylov_allocations.jl` (948 kB per GMRES solve at $n = 2000$ against 0) | repeated solves (every Newton step, every adjoint, every sweep) allocate afresh today |
| Q12 | **Allocation budgets as tests.** After warm-up, each inner kernel's allocations are tested: zero, or a stated bound with its reason. The kernels are the Gaussian message, the constraint message, the plate block kernel, `solve!` on a cache (Q11) and one flooding sweep on a fixed graph. Use AllocCheck.jl's `@check_allocs` where it applies and `@allocated` after warm-up elsewhere | proposed | Q11 | medium | baseline above (three test files check allocations) | an allocation-free kernel stays so only if a test says so |
| Q13 | **Benchmarks as a gate.** Each package gets a `benchmark/benchmarks.jl` (BenchmarkTools `SUITE`) covering time, allocations and first-call latency on the workloads of paths A, B and C. CI compares every pull request with `dev` and `master` (AirspeedVelocity.jl or PkgBenchmark.jl) and reports regressions over a stated threshold. The `bench/` scripts stay as the recorded studies; each of their notes states Julia version, thread count, machine and commit. Takes up T2 | proposed | — | high | baseline above; T2 | a regression is found today only if someone reruns a study and reads the note |
| Q14 | **Profiles of the three paths.** One profile per path of a representative workload: a Kalman chain or grid (A), a relation-learner query with its gradient (B), and an EM run with a switching factor (C). Each is a script in `bench/` writing a flame graph (`Profile`, PProf.jl) and its allocation profile (`Profile.Allocs`) to `~/.cache/lenticulum/`. Its note says where the time goes, and it is repeated when a path changes. `bench/sweep_profile.jl` and `regime_plate_profile.jl` are the start | proposed | — | medium | baseline above | optimisation is guided by measurement, not guesses, and the profile is what a reviewer asks for first |
| Q15 | **First-call latency.** PrecompileTools workloads in each package (a small graph of each path through inference, the free energy and one gradient). `bench/structure_compile.jl` and `jit_latency.jl` become part of Q13's gate. Measure what the workloads save and what they add to precompile time. Also measure whether the first call on a new structure (2.04–4.53 s against 0.011–0.039 s warm, baseline) comes from code specialised to the structure (I1, I2) or from generic code that a workload would cover | proposed | — | high | `bench/structure_compile.md` | the maintainer expects dynamic compilation of factor graphs to be a production problem; this row measures how much of it the Julia side can remove before Q16 |
| Q16 | **The factor graph as a compiled program, with Sophia as the compiler backend.** Separate the structure (compiled once) from the data (per call). I2's typed execution plan is lowered to a program that Reactant can trace: no scalar indexing (Q7), no data-dependent Julia control flow inside a sweep (fixed iteration counts, or `@trace` loops), and types concrete throughout (Q2). The first test traces one sweep and one free-energy gradient of a Kalman chain with Reactant on the CPU and compares them with the Julia result, as `lib/VariationalDiffusion.jl/test/reactant/` does for its backend. The compiled executable then becomes a cache entry under the derived program's id in Sophia (Sophia B-093, E-7: a Reactant executable restored in a fresh process, bit-identical, saving about 12 s of the first gradient). Alternatives to measure on the way: `juliac --trim` for a fixed graph, and PrecompileTools alone (Q15) | proposed | I2, Q7 | medium | the maintainer, 2026-11-24 ("in production there will be problems with dynamically compiling the factor graph"); Sophia `AD as a Materialization Case.md` | this is a conjecture until Q15 measures it; the plan makes the graph compilable whichever backend wins |
| Q17 | **Own code against the ecosystem.** Each reimplementation gets a recorded reason and a comparison, or is replaced: ImplicitSolvers' GMRES and MINRES and VariationalDiffusion's CG, Lanczos and log-determinant against Krylov.jl and KrylovKit.jl (P40); `solve_root`, Broyden and Anderson, and `solve_columns`' Newton and trust region (P39) against NonlinearSolve.jl; the finite differences against FiniteDiff.jl and FiniteDifferences.jl; ImplicitLayers' DEQs and neural ODEs against DeepEquilibriumNetworks.jl and SciMLSensitivity.jl. The comparison covers results on the oracle tests, time, allocations, backends and devices | proposed | — | medium | [[Shared Solver Layer]] "Why not Krylov.jl" | consolidation is fine with a reason; P14 showed that reasons were not recorded |
