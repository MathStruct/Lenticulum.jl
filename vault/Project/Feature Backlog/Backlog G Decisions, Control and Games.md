#overview

> Section G of the [[Feature Backlog]]: control as inference, owned scalarisations in games, open games (asked for 2026-11-16). Open rows only; a row that is
> done moves to [[Backlog Done 2026-11]] with its id. Status, priority, ids and the column *needs*
> are explained in [[Feature Backlog]], which also holds the dependency graph.

> Sources: original to this repository; the sources of each row are in its *source* column.

Daniel asked how far the library is from reinforcement learning "fully realized": compositional
multiplayer open games, explicit best responses, Nash equilibria. The rows below are the answer as
a list, from a reading of the code and of [[GANs as Two Factors]] §4–§5 and [[The Two-Part Diagram]]
§3 and §6; nothing was run or designed for them. They come in three tiers. G1–G4 are one agent and
one free energy (the cooperative row of [[The Two-Part Diagram]]): new factors and one message
rule on the engine as it is. G5–G8 build on P16 (owned scalarisations): once a parameter block
descends its own scalarisation there is no one $F$, and three things that rely on it change. G9
and G10 are a layer beside the statistical game, in which the backward pass carries a best
response; it would be a package of its own with its own solve entry, so that `infer!` and its
promises stay as they are. Whether a `Polarity` (or F8) can carry ownership, and whether a cluster
node can serve as the solver of a stage game, were not checked. G11 is the belief type those rows
need on top: an epistemic belief over hypotheses (types, strategies, models), not over the states
of one space.

| G8 | Entropy-regularised games: ownership extended from parameter keys to decision variables, so that each owner's belief over its action minimises its own free energy (expected payoff minus its own entropy, at a temperature $\tau$). A first version by sampling (Daniel's route, 2026-11-16): `GibbsSampler` with a conditional per owner, each decision variable drawn from $\exp(-u_i/\tau)$ of its own component with the others pinned (logit-response dynamics); oracle for it: a potential game, whose chain has the Gibbs distribution of the potential as its stationary law | proposed | P16 | low | Daniel, 2026-11-16; `lib/Mycelium.jl/src/sampling.md` | the step from P16 that reuses the most: vector energies, beliefs and message updates stay, and only who answers to which component changes. Nash equilibria are its $\tau \to 0$ limit (G3). Whether the per-owner updates converge is the question of G6 again, for beliefs. The sampler returns to a statistical game (one joint, one free energy) only for potential games; from the literature, not run here: in a general game the per-owner conditionals belong to no joint and the chain's stationary law is not the quantal-response equilibrium (a product of strategies), and in a zero-sum game the chain is not reversible. Today `_conditional!` draws from the marginal under all adjacent factors, and the variables not sampled are integrated out under the one $F$: both would change |
| G9 | Open games as a type beside the statistical game, in a package of its own: a forward play (a Bayesian lens, reusing `BayesianLens` and `pushforward`), a backward coutility, a best-response relation on strategy profiles, sequential and parallel composition, and an equilibrium predicate ([arXiv:1603.04641](https://arxiv.org/abs/1603.04641); Bayesian: [arXiv:1910.03656](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.03656)). Oracles: the pure and mixed equilibria of finite games by enumeration, and composition against the composite's equilibria on small games | proposed | — | low | Daniel, 2026-11-16; [[GANs as Two Factors]] §5–§6 | a best response is a relation on profiles, not a message, and an equilibrium is a fixed point of it, not a minimum of a functional; `lens.jl` and `statistical_game.jl` are built on `invert` with a prior, so this is a sibling, not a subtype. Computing equilibria is a solver question apart from checking them. G8 at small $\tau$ should agree with it where both apply. With Dirac or sample beliefs a graph is already the parametrised lens underneath an open game (play forwards, energies backwards, `ps` as the strategy profile; [Open Game](https://mathstruct.org/CategoryTheory-ML-Wiki/Open-Game): a parametrised lens plus a best-response relation), so the play half may need no new type and the new part is the relation: the owners of P16 and what each answers to. Read from the definitions, not tried |
| G10 | Markov games: a backward sweep over G1's unrolled horizon in which each step solves a stage game (G9, or G8 at a temperature) and passes one value per player backwards. Oracle: Shapley's value iteration for zero-sum stage games | proposed | G1, G9 | low | Daniel, 2026-11-16 | Bellman's backup with an owner per action; the multiplayer case of G1 and G3 |
| G11 | An epistemic belief type, `EpistemicBelief{B} <: AbstractBelief` with `hypotheses::Vector{B}` and `weights::Vector{Float64}`: a belief over arbitrary hypotheses — types, strategies, whole models, other players' beliefs — not only over the states of one space. Pooling it with a factor that scores each hypothesis is the Bayesian update of the weights, $w_k \\propto w_k\\, p(e \\mid h_k)$. Oracle: that update checked against `CategoricalBelief` on finite labelled sets, and pooling two epistemic beliefs on shared hypotheses against enumeration | proposed | — | low | Daniel, 2026-11-16 | `MixtureBelief` (`lib/LenticulumCore.jl/src/open_model.jl`) needs its components to be `AbstractBelief`s and stores log-weights; `CategoricalBelief` needs a finite label set. The message store and the free energy need a branch for the new type, as `ExtendedGaussian` got in P4. Goes better with P16, G8 and G9; needs nothing open |
