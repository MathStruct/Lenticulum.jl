implementation

Executes belief schedules, with optional recording of committed residual tasks for the separately defined realised-schedule derivative.

Sources: Riegler, Kirkelund, Manchon, Badiu & Fleury, Merging Belief Propagation and the Mean Field Approximation: A Free Energy Approach, IEEE Trans. Inf. Theory 59(1), 2013 (mean-field nodes). code: passing.jl

Bibliography: Riegler et al. 2013

Theory (CT-ML wiki): Bayesian Inversion · Lens

Implements: executing a schedule. step! → sweep! → propagate! → infer!. Theory: Messages are Inversions, Loopy Message Passing.

step! is where the identity lives

For a :to_variable task:

Five lines, and every one of them is a named thing from the paper: the polarity is the input/output split, the prior is the π of cπ′​ (the cavity), the inputs are the y, and factor_message is the inversion with π divided out: the likelihood site μf→v​ with cπ′​(y)=π⊙μf→v​, not the posterior itself (Messages are Inversions.md, LP-019 item 11).

factor_message’s generic implementation is assemble then likelihood_message, which defaults to invert for lenses whose inversion is already extrinsic (L-ADR-0006). Structural factors override it directly, because for them the lens is trivial and building one would be ceremony.

Mean-field nodes (2026-11-01)

Every factor node carries a message rule, chosen with factor!(b, name, f; rule): BeliefPropagation() (the default, everything above) or MeanField(). For a mean-field node step! replaces the two cavity lines by the other variables’ full marginals:

Nothing else changes. Belief-propagation nodes still receive cavities, and these include the mean-field nodes’ messages. So a graph with both kinds runs the hybrid of Riegler, Kirkelund, Manchon, Badiu & Fleury (Merging Belief Propagation and the Mean Field Approximation: A Free Energy Approach, IEEE Trans. Inf. Theory 59(1), 2013). Its stationary points are exact within the belief-propagation part and factorised across the mean-field nodes. A typical use is a dense coupling factor, such as a sum node over many inputs, on mean field, with structured factors on belief propagation around it.

  • Iterate a sequential schedule. A mean-field node’s message to one neighbour changes the marginals its other messages read, so one tree sweep is not a fixed point. In-place sweeps give coordinate ascent.
  • mean_field_message has no generic method. The expectation needs the factor’s form, so a factor opts in. Lenticulum implements it for LinearConstraintFactor (the BP message with each input at its mean, since logf is quadratic) and for SwitchingConstraintFactor.
  • The graph free energy is the merged one (2026-11-02, L-SPEC-005 The Hybrid Free Energy). Riegler et al. (2013), eq. (17): a mean-field node contributes mean_field_energy, its average energy under the product of its variables’ marginals with no entropy, and the variables’ counting numbers count belief-propagation neighbours only. free_energy_gradient and parameter_step train such graphs at fixed beliefs and marginals. Before this the free energy refused them, and learning was EM outside the graph.
  • Convergence is measured on the messages, as for loopy BP. Mixtures of very narrow components have a rounding floor in belief_distance, about 10−10 to 10−8 at width 10−8, so set tol above it.

Tested in test/message_rules.jl: the default rule; a recording factor that sees the marginal (prior pooled with its own earlier message), not the cavity; the refusal for a factor without mean_field_energy; and the missing-method error. The end-to-end check, a hybrid graph against an independent structured mean field, is in Lenticulum’s test/switching_constraint.jl.

sweep! executors have different semantics

  • Generic (sequential, tree, forward-backward): executes in place. Later tasks see earlier results within the same sweep, so information crosses a whole path per sweep.
  • FloodingSchedule: double buffered. Every task is computed with commit = false against the previous sweep’s messages, and all results are written afterwards. Information crosses one edge per sweep.
  • ResidualSchedule: probes every legal task, commits the largest residual, then repeats up to its task budget. Probe state updates are discarded; committed updates advance state. Ties prefer the oldest message, then task order.

The trade is not just speed. On a loopy graph the sequential order is a modelling choice made without noticing. Flooding removes message-order dependence for stateless factors; Lux state still threads in task order, so stateful evaluations can depend on that order.

Implementation difficulties

1. _inputs is O(df​) per channel, so step! is O(df2​)

_inputs does a findfirst over the factor’s edges for each requested channel. For a factor of degree d that is O(d2) per message and O(d3) per factor per sweep. Irrelevant for degree 2–5, wrong for a factor with a hundred channels. A precomputed channel → edge map per factor in FactorGraph would fix it and is the obvious next optimisation.

2. ps/st threading is by name and is not validated

_subtree(ps, name) returns NamedTuple() when the key is absent, so a typo in a factor’s name silently yields empty parameters rather than an error. That is the single most likely way to get a confusingly wrong answer from this file: a factor trains on nothing and nobody says so.

_setsubtree also merges into the state tree on every message, allocating a fresh NamedTuple each time. Correct (states must not be mutated in place if the caller holds the old one) but wasteful in the inner loop.

A validate_parameters(g, ps) pre-flight check — every learnable factor has an entry, no entries without a factor — is missing and would be cheap.

3. Damping is applied after the message is computed, and only to :to_variable

step! damps the outgoing belief against the previous one on that edge. Variable → factor messages are never damped, which is the usual convention (they are deterministic pools of already-damped inputs) but is a convention, not a theorem.

Damping is implemented for numeric Diracs, Gaussian canonical parameters, categoricals and compatible fixed-component mixtures/samples (messages). It remains a silent no-op for unsupported belief pairs. propagate! does not check can_damp before promising damping, so a caller can pass damping = 0.3 and get none.

4. propagate! special-cases TreeSchedule by type

With damping the shortcut is wrong even on a tree: a damped sweep keeps part of each stored message, so from a warm start one sweep is not the fixed point. A unary source moved from mean 0 to 10 and swept once with damping = 0.5 gave mean 5 and the report “exact” (LP-019 item 5). Damped tree schedules are now iterated and checked like any other schedule.

The claim “one sweep is exact” is true only if the graph really is a tree — which tree_schedule enforced at construction. So the invariant is carried by the type, which is sound but implicit: a hand-constructed TreeSchedule(inward, outward) over a loopy graph would get a false “exact” report. A TreeSchedule carrying the graph it was built for, or a construction-only inner constructor, would close that hole.

5. infer! returns four things

(marginals, report, st, store). The store is returned because bethe_free_energy needs it, but a four-tuple is a poor interface. An InferenceResult struct with named fields is the obvious improvement and was skipped only to avoid inventing a type before knowing what else belongs in it.

6. Cotangents are not here, deliberately

Gradients with respect to parameters are not belief messages. They are computed once per parameter step by the separate gradient_couplings driver, with finite schedule replay or an opt-in converged-message adjoint for non-diagonal boundaries. optimiser_step remains separate from step!; parameters are not updated inside an inference sweep.

LP-011 implementation (2026-10-27)

Values crossing detached edges use the core stop primitive, in both directions. Factor parameters are pinned according to graph freezing and traits before ordinary message evaluation. The optional ChainRules infer! rule replays a fixed realised static sweep count without damping; it returns no state/report cotangents. It does not differentiate convergence decisions. The former statement that all coupling gradients are unimplemented is superseded by the separate parameter driver.

Evidence: lib/MyceliumLearning.jl/test/gradient_couplings.jl and test/gradient_couplings.jl. API, mixed Jacobians, costs and unsupported combinations are specified in gradient_couplings and L-SPEC-002 Stop Gradient.

Batch C recording and replay (2026-10-28)

The optional record sink on residual sweep! and propagate! appends only committed tasks. With no sink, task choices and arithmetic are unchanged. The ChainRules infer! rule records that reference pass and replays its fixed choices; flooding replays double buffering and all schedules include outgoing damping. No report, graph or state cotangent is returned. gradient_couplings specifies the piecewise derivative and deterministic/functional-factor requirement. The separate implicit driver verifies every synchronous BP row before its adjoint solve, rather than trusting TreeSchedule’s ordinary one-sweep report.

Evidence: test/graph_adjoint.jl, test/graph_adjoint_devices.jl.

Parameter keys and batched messages (2026-11-02)

step! reads and writes ps[key] and st[key] with the node’s parameter key (plates.md), which is its name unless tied, so difficulty 2 above now reads “by key”. factor_messages(factors, target, polarity, inputs, priors, ps, st) is the batched form of factor_message for tied replicas; the default calls factor_message for each, and a plate sweep (PlateSchedule) calls it for each group it can batch.

Convergence on marginals (2026-11-03)

propagate!(...; criterion = :marginals) compares every variable’s marginal before and after a sweep by moments (_moment_distance in messages.jl: means and covariances of proper Gaussians, mixture weights and components, total variation for categoricals, belief_distance otherwise). The message residual of a mixture of components of width 10−8 stops falling near 10−8 because it is measured in natural parameters, whose entries are of order 108; the moments have no such floor, so test/switching_constraint.jl converges its hybrid at tol = 1e-10. It is the default for a HybridSchedule and for plate schedules lifted from one; it costs one marginal per variable per sweep.

factor_messages on blocks (2026-11-03)

A second method of factor_messages takes inputs as a NamedTuple of BeliefBlocks and returns one block (blocks.md). The default takes each replica’s column, calls the method on vectors and packs the result with belief_block, so a factor type adds block arithmetic by adding a method of that form and nothing else changes. DataFactor (in factors.jl) returns its data as one point block.

Expectation propagation and mixture reduction (2026-11-03)

Two more rules (Extending Mycelium §4, L-SPEC-008 Expectation Propagation and Mixture Reduction). Both compute the belief-propagation message first, from the same cavities, and change what is sent. step! calls _rule_message(rule, ...), which is factor_message for belief propagation.

ExpectationPropagationRule(). With mt​ the belief-propagation message to channel t and πt​ the target’s cavity (the excluded marginal, already the prior argument), the node sends

mtEP​=proj[πt​mt​]/πt​,

the projection matching moments (Minka 2001). Three hooks, all in Mycelium: ep_message(m, π) (a message that is not a mixture is in its family and passes unchanged; a point cavity, a pinned variable, makes the message irrelevant and it passes too), ep_project (a mixture of Gaussians: its total mean and covariance) and ep_divide (Gaussians: subtract canonical parameters). A site whose precision is not positive definite, possibly indefinite, is declared improper. An update is skipped, keeping the edge’s message, when the cavity is a Gaussian that is not proper, when a component of the tilted belief is not proper, or when an input to the factor has a negative direction (left by an earlier negative site). Skipping keeps the iteration defined; it is a common remedy, not a rule we quote from Minka’s paper. The first skip on a run warns. On a Gaussian graph nothing leaves the family and the rule is belief propagation (tested to rounding).

The name is not ExpectationPropagation: Lenticulum already exports that, the moment-matching inversion method of a LikelihoodFactor (likelihood.jl, Gauss–Hermite quadrature inside one factor). The rule generalises it to any factor whose message leaves the family.

MixtureReduction(M). The message is the exact one with every mixture input expanded (mixture_message, a factor hook; belief propagation collapses those inputs to moments), then reduced to at most M components (reduce_message; Lenticulum’s method is Runnalls’ merge, reduce_mixture). Lenticulum implements mixture_message for LinearConstraintFactor and SwitchingConstraintFactor (switching.md §9). A factor without it refuses a mixture input on such a node; one with only Gaussian inputs takes factor_message.

One tree sweep. propagate! used to stop a TreeSchedule after one undamped sweep whatever the rules. That is a fixed point when every message depends on upstream messages only: belief propagation, and MixtureReduction, whose messages are exact for reduced inputs. An EP message reads the target’s cavity and a mean-field message the marginals, so a graph with either is now iterated (_tree_exact). Before, a mean-field graph swept with a TreeSchedule was reported exact after one sweep; the hybrid tests use HybridSchedule, so no test met it.

Free energy. An EP node counts like a belief-propagation node: its local free energy at its incoming messages, and an entropy at each of its variables (free_energy.md). A MixtureReduction node is refused there: no free energy we know of has its fixed points as stationary points, and the factors’ local free energies would collapse the mixtures they were sent.

Tests: Mycelium’s own (lib/Mycelium.jl/test/message_rules.jl, last testset) run a prior and three likelihood mixtures without Lenticulum: belief propagation pools them into 8 components in one sweep, the EP graph is iterated and each site times its cavity has the tilted moments to 10−10, EP edges count in bp_degree, and the refusals. The end-to-end ones are Lenticulum’s (test/mixture_rules.jl):

  • Clutter problem, D=1, w=0.5, prior variance 100, clutter variance 10, data drawn from the model (Xoshiro(3)). Belief propagation is exact on this star: its marginal holds all 2N components and matches the enumerated posterior to 10−12 (N=8,12). The EP rule reaches the fixed point of a hand-written Minka loop to 10−10 on a tree and on a sequential schedule. At N=12 its mean is within 6⋅10−4 of the exact one and its variance within 2%; at N=8 the mean is off by 0.10. At N=4 the undamped iteration oscillates, as the hand loop does, and the rule follows that loop step for step.
  • A location-mixture likelihood (yi​=x−bki​​+ε, six points, tied switching factors on EP): the gradient of the free energy at fixed messages equals the derivative of the free energy at the re-converged fixed point, to 10−7 relative, for an offset and a weight. So the free energy of EP nodes is stationary in the messages at a fixed point, which is what lets the fixed-belief gradient train such graphs. This is a numerical check, not a theorem quoted.
  • A switching linear chain, T=5, K=2 (xt+1​=xt​+βkt​​+ε, yt​=xt​+δ), against enumeration of the 16 switch sequences: the largest error of a marginal mean or variance falls with the budget, 0.16, 0.011, 9.6⋅10−4, 1.0⋅10−4 for M=1,2,4,8, and is 1.6⋅10−15 at M=16, where nothing is merged. Belief propagation gives the M=2 result: it collapses the input and sends K=2 components.

Difficulties:

  1. The exact reference needed a new product. Belief propagation on the clutter star multiplies likelihood messages, mixtures whose components are unnormalised exponentials (GaussianBelief(η, Λ, true)). combine_with_overlap needed a proper first operand, so the reference threw. The product of two unnormalised exponentials is exact in canonical form (gaussian.md, “Products of improper components”).
  2. The first EP sweep has no cavities. On a tree schedule’s inward pass a likelihood node can fire before the prior’s message has reached the variable, so its cavity is uninformative and its tilted belief, the likelihood mixture itself, has improper components. Such an update is skipped; with no previous message the edge stays empty, and the outward pass fills it.
  3. A message to a pointed variable stays a mixture. On the location-mixture graph the messages to the observed yi​ are not projected: their cavities are points. That is harmless (a point dominates), but an assertion that “every message is Gaussian” is wrong there; the test checks the marginal of x.

Skipped updates under flooding and residual schedules (2026-11-04, LP-023)

An expectation-propagation update without a proper cavity is skipped: the edge keeps its message. step! with commit = true already did that, but the two schedules that compute without committing did not. FloodingSchedule stored the returned TrivialBelief() on an edge that had no message, and ResidualSchedule selected the skipped task (residual Inf) and could not commit it. _step! now also returns whether the update was skipped; flooding leaves such an edge as it is, and the residual schedule never selects it. step! is unchanged. Test: test/mixture_rules.jl.

Compilation per structure (2026-11-04)

ps and st are graph-wide trees with a key per parameter key, so their type changes with every new graph structure. step!, _step!, every sweep! and the plate group steps (_step_planned!, _step_blocks!) read them only through _subtree by the node’s key, and are now @nospecialize in them; the factor’s own methods still specialise on its small subtree. Measured with SnoopCompile (a scratch script; examples/structure_edits.jl’s graphs, 200 points): a structure with three switches, after one with two had compiled, spent 9.06 s inferring its first two sweeps, 6.93 s of it in _step! for a tree of 201 keys; with @nospecialize, 1.14 s. Its first sweeps took 8.77 s before and 0.70 s after. The example’s greedy search fell from 192–200 s to 163.8 s with gradient fits and from 177 s to 143 s with EM, the same paths and scores. A ForwardDiff gradient still compiles per structure (3.7 s), since the dual numbers’ chunk follows the parameter count; EM does not use it.

Extended 2026-11-05 to the graph-wide free-energy entry points (scalar_free_energy, bethe_free_energy, factor_free_energies, _factor_terms, _factor_terms_rows), the cluster code outside AD (_cluster_joint, the cluster’s factor_message and factor_belief, the batched factor_messages, factor_beliefs, _batch_joints, _joint_plan, _cluster_batchable) and EM (moment_statistics, em_step, the statistics’ ClusterForm). The functions AD differentiates stay specialised (their dual-number types change per structure anyway). Measured in a scratch script on examples/structure_edits.jl’s graphs: the free energy, em_step and a five-step fit_em on a new structure took 1.8 s, now 1.14 s (0.31 s warm); the warm per-call times are unchanged. The structure search with EM went from 34.9 s to 31.3 s, the same path and scores.
