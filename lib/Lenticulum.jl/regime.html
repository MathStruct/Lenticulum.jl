regime

implementation

RegimeFactor: a switching linear constraint whose coefficients and noise depend on the outcome as well as its right-hand side, 0=∑i​Ai,k​xi​−bk​+εk​, εk​∼N(0,Qk​), P(k)=wk​. It is stated only through a cluster form with per-outcome A and Q, exact inside a cluster node, and outside one it is a one-factor cluster.

Sources: code: regime.jl, switching.jl; Mycelium lib/Mycelium.jl/src/cluster.jl (as_cluster, per-outcome forms); tests: test/regime.jl

Theory (CT-ML wiki): Switching Constraint Factors, Composing Stochastic Factors

1. The factor

f(x1​,…,xn​,k)=wk​N(∑i​Ai,k​xi​−bk​; 0, Qk​),k∈{1,…,K}.

SwitchingConstraintFactor (switching.md) is the case Ai,k​=Ai​, Qk​=Q, the “location-shift” case, and listed the general one under what it does not do: with Ak​ or Qk​ depending on the outcome the outcomes’ Gaussians have different precisions. Instances are mixtures of regressions (experiment E1’s two crossing lines, Az,k​=[−ak​  1]), Gaussian mixtures with one covariance per component, mixtures of factor analysers and switching linear dynamics with one transition matrix per regime.

The parameters are A (a NamedTuple of m×di​×K arrays), b (m×K) and logw. The noise is fixed (one covariance per regime, or one shared), or with learn_noise = true a parameter L (m×m×K) with Qk​=Lk​Lk⊤​, Lk​ the strictly lower triangle of L[:, :, k] plus the exponential of its diagonal; the strict upper triangle is unused and gets a zero gradient. regime_covariances(f, ps) returns the Qk​.

Output channel. output = :y fixes the coefficient of y at output_coeff in every regime and drops it from the parameters. The factor is then a normalised conditional density of y, a mixture of regressions. Without it, learning every coefficient has no lower bound with learn_noise (LP-022, test/lp022.jl).

2. How it is computed

The factor implements only Mycelium.cluster_form: residual ∑i​Ai,k​xi​−bk​ with per-outcome A and Q arrays (cluster.md, “Per-outcome coefficients and noise”). That gives:

  • Inside a cluster node, exact inference: assignments are grouped into precision classes, one per combination of the varying factors’ outcomes, and each class is one Gaussian elimination.
  • Outside a cluster, its messages, belief and Bethe free energy are those of the one-factor cluster Mycelium.as_cluster(f) over its own channels (parameters wrapped as (factor = ps,)). The message to the outcome is ∝wk​∫f; to a continuous channel a mixture of K Gaussians with precisions Λk​=At,k⊤​Qˉ​k−1​At,k​ after the other inputs are eliminated. The free energy is the cluster’s: Eb​[−logf]−H(b) with b the factor times its incoming messages, closed form given the outcome.

3. What it does not do

  • Flat inputs are integrated out in the diffuse limit since 2026-11-04 (cluster.md, “Flat directions”), provided every regime leaves equally many directions flat; otherwise the message is refused. A mixture input is collapsed to its moments.
  • Improper messages with several precisions are sent (since 2026-11-04; refused before): each improper component is the unnormalised exponential its weight multiplies, which is how Mycelium’s products read it, so components with different precisions are weighted consistently (test/cluster_flat.jl).
  • No mean-field, mixture-expansion or EP rules, no block arithmetic over a plate: a plate row of regime factors (or of cluster nodes holding them) is batched through tied one-factor clusters (factor_messages, factor_beliefs, belief_free_energies, local_free_energies for a vector of RegimeFactors; cluster.md, “Per-outcome coefficients and noise”), which shares the per-class factorisations but still builds each replica’s message as a belief, not a block.
  • No game interface (assemble, invert): only the Mycelium factor methods.

4. Checked by

test/regime.jl:

  • a mixture of factor analysers (x∼N(0,I), three regimes with different Wk​, μk​, Qk​) in one cluster: F=−logp(z) to 10−12 relative, the regime posterior and each regime’s posterior mean and covariance of x to 10−12;
  • the free-energy gradient in A, b, logw and L against central differences of −logp(z), to 10−6, with ForwardDiff and Zygote;
  • outside a cluster: a Gaussian mixture with per-component covariance (free energy, message to k, gradient in b against the closed form), the message to a continuous channel (each component’s Λk​, ηk​ and weight against the closed form), the refusal of improper components with different precisions, and the message to z with per-regime noise;
  • over a plate of four points: the batched path, F=−∑i​logp(zi​), the gradient in b.

test/regime_plate.jl: batched against per-replica messages, beliefs, energies and gradients on random instances; a plated clustered mixture of factor analysers against the closed form; a plated mixture of regressions without clusters against the unplated graph, EM included (cluster.md gives the tolerances).

examples/regime_lines.jl (regime_lines.md): E1’s crossing lines with one noise level per line, 300 points, fitted by the free-energy gradient on the graph, reaches EM’s maximum likelihood to 2⋅10−8 in −logp; one shared noise level is 53.5 worse.

5. Difficulties

  1. A free-energy check that could not be made. A first test put the prior of x outside the cluster and compared the graph’s Bethe free energy with −logp(z). The cluster’s message to x is a mixture, and Mycelium has no entropy for a mixture variable belief (switching.md §5), so the graph has no free energy there. The message is tested directly instead.
  2. Zygote and generators. The covariances were first built from a generator splatted into cat, which Zygote has no adjoint for; a map works.
  3. An ambiguous noise. A real vector is a diagonal (as for the other factors), so per-regime noise is a vector of matrices or an m×m×K array, not a vector of scalars.

Element type

Computation follows Julia promotion of the parameters, noise, incoming messages and data it reads; data and parameters are not converted to force a result type. Constants and numerical hyperparameters are cast at use.

The output coefficient is stored exact when given exact (I) and takes the parameters’ floating type at use (P34: constraint.md, “The fixed output coefficient”), so with learn_noise = true the default noise = 0.1 no longer widens a Float32 factor. The noise tensor follows promotion of its regime covariances, and initial noise coordinates/log weights use the noise type. The lower-triangular mask is Boolean. Messages and energies delegate to Mycelium cluster inference, which keeps the element type of its forms and messages since P32. Initial coefficient/offset callbacks continue to decide their own output types.

Checks: lib/Lenticulum.jl/test/p31_element_type.jl. The implementation is not a claim of coverage for every mixed precision, derivative, or device configuration.

Element types (Q4)

Fixed regime covariances are converted to the type of the parameter tree before building the cluster form. The cluster uses the incoming beliefs when promoting that form. The BigFloat case with every continuous channel latent still needs Mycelium’s diffuse cluster eigendecomposition, as recorded in test/eltypes.jl.

Device arrays (Q7)

Adapt moves per-outcome covariances and any fixed coefficient. Regime messages and local free energy use Mycelium’s continuous device buffers and diffuse host spectral helper.

LP-094 part 2 (2026-11-27, element types from the review of Q19)

Findings 5 and 6. The regime factor shares _logweights and _coeffs/_fixed_at with the constraint factors: cluster_form now passes regime_covariances(f, ps) to _logweights (an integer logw no longer widens), and _fixed_output follows the new _fixed_at(C, f, ps) signature, so an integer parameter set with a declared output takes the stored noise’s float type instead of Float64. Tests: lib/Lenticulum.jl/test/lp094.jl, findings 5 and 6.

Q20 device constructors

The direct constructor packs per-regime covariances on the device of its numeric input. It validates each covariance before writing its slice.
