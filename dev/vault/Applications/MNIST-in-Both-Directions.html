MNIST in Both Directions

MNIST in Both Directions

13 Oct 202610 min read

  • application
  • experiment

application experiment

One implicit diffusion model of (image, label) on MNIST, queried in both directions: clamp the image and the free label is a classification; clamp the label and the free image is a digit; clamp half an image and both the rest and the label come out at once. The demo of “one relation, polarity chosen at query time” that everyone recognises. Run (§6), with a split result. Image → label works: 96.1% by root finding, the same as a diffusion classifier with the same network (95.8%). Label → image does not: with this model the clamped label barely affects the generated image (8–25% of samples show the requested digit).

Sources: original to this vault (experiment design); LeCun, Bottou, Bengio & Haffner, Gradient-Based Learning Applied to Document Recognition, Proc. IEEE 1998 (MNIST); Grathwohl et al., Your Classifier is Secretly an Energy Based Model…, ICLR 2020 (JEM); Li et al., Your Diffusion Model is Secretly a Zero-Shot Classifier, ICCV 2023; Clark & Jaini, Text-to-Image Diffusion Models are Zero-Shot Classifiers, NeurIPS 2023; Bao et al., One Transformer Fits All Distributions in Multi-Modal Diffusion at Scale, ICML 2023 (UniDiffuser); Lugmayr et al., RePaint, CVPR 2022; code: implicit.jl, answers.jl, energy_network.jl

Bibliography: LeCun et al. 1998 · Grathwohl et al. 2020 · Li et al. 2023 · Clark & Jaini 2023 · Bao et al. 2023 · Lugmayr et al. 2022

Theory: Implicit Diffusion Learners · Inference Signatures · Mixture Belief · Backward Signatures

1. The setup

The joint state is z=(x,y) with the image x∈R784 and the label y∈R10, a one-hot vector (possibly scaled, so that the label is not drowned by 784 pixel coordinates in the denoising loss). Train ordinary denoising on the concatenation, with an EnergyNetwork, so that answers get weights (§3). The direction is chosen only at query time, by the precisions ρ:

queryclamped (ρ=∞)freecomputed by
classifyxyimplicit_roots / implicit_mixture on 10 coordinates
the most typical “3”yximplicit_infer: a mode, §2
draw a “3”yximplicit_sample, the clamped sampler (signature #6 of Inference Signatures)
inpaint and classifyhalf of xthe other half, and yimplicit_infer

The last row is the one no ordinary classifier or generator does, and it costs nothing extra.

2. What each direction returns

Classification is cheap: a 10-dimensional solve with a 10×10 Jacobian block. The answers are roots near the one-hot vectors; with an energy, implicit_mixture’s Laplace-mass weights wk​∝e−E(zk​)det(Σk​)1/2 are class probabilities, and their calibration is a measurable result (reliability diagram against a softmax classifier). An ambiguous digit should give two roots of comparable weight, which is the point of returning a mixture rather than an argmax.

Generation by root finding returns a mode of the smoothed p(x∣y): one deterministic prototype per basin, not a sample. Whether the modes look like digits or like blurry class averages depends on the smoothing scale (the field’s noise levels, Implicit Diffusion Learners §5), and is an experiment of its own. Multi-start from noise finds several modes (styles of “7”), but with no frequencies attached unless the weights are computed in 784 dimensions.

Sampling needs the reverse SDE with the label coordinates held fixed, the replacement trick of RePaint: the same network, no retraining, but the sampler path, not the implicit solver.

3. How this differs from the prior work

how it classifieshow it generates
JEM (Grathwohl et al. 2020)the logits of a classifier read as an energy of (x,y)SGLD on that energy
diffusion classifiers (Li et al. 2023; Clark & Jaini 2023)Bayes’ rule over per-class denoising errors: one Monte-Carlo pass per classthe diffusion model itself
UniDiffuser (Bao et al. 2023)one noise level per modality, every conditional from one networkthe same
thisroot finding in label space, with Laplace weights as probabilitiesmodes by root finding; samples by a clamped reverse SDE

The claim worth testing is the cost line: a 10-dimensional solve against ten Monte-Carlo estimates of a denoising loss. The accuracy will most likely sit below a plain classifier’s ~99%. The selling point is one model for three queries, with uncertainty, not the benchmark.

4. Interfaces that have to exist first

  1. Partial Jacobians — done. prior_jacobian(…; coords = free) computes only JFF​, and the pullback gets JFC⊤​λ from one VJP (prior_vjp). On a 794-dimensional MLP with 10 free coordinates a solver iteration went from about 26 s to 0.04 s (implicit §6).
  2. Batching and matrix-free solves (Open Problems in Implicit Diffusion Learning I5) — done: one network call per noise level (batched = true), and linsolve = KrylovSolve() (GMRES, Lanczos stability, stochastic Lanczos logdetΣ). With the image free (784 of 794 coordinates) a solve takes 1.1 s (krylov). Many queries at once: done, implicit_infer_many evaluates all unfinished queries in one network call per noise level (implicit §7).
  3. A clamped sampler for ε- and energy networks (I1) — done: implicit_sample(m, z₀, ρ, ps, st; n, steps, resample, rng), the reverse diffusion with the hard-clamped coordinates replaced at every step and RePaint’s resampling (sampler).
  4. A categorical answer — done: implicit_classify starts from every class code and reads the mixture of roots as a CategoricalBelief. The mass of roots far from every code is reported as unassigned. On three closed-form classes the probabilities are within 0.04 of the exact posterior (implicit §8).
  5. Energy networks at this size, on a GPU — done: device = (to = gpu_device(), back = cpu_device()) with ad = SecondOrder(AutoForwardDiff(), AutoZygote()). At this network size a training step takes 6 ms and a classification iteration 1.8 ms per query, in batches of 1000 (energy_network §8). Enzyme and Reactant are not needed for it.
  6. Optional: the backward signatures (Backward Signatures), only if the generative model is fine-tuned discriminatively through the classification query. Pure denoising training needs none of it. The solver level is there: Zygote.gradient differentiates through implicit_infer by its rrule.

The data loader (MLDatasets.jl) belongs in the tutorial’s environment, never in a package’s dependencies.

Found by the experiment (§6): still needed for the generative direction and for graded answers. These are open problems I12 and I13 in Open Problems in Implicit Diffusion Learning.

  1. A noise level per block of coordinates (UniDiffuser), in the predictor, training, the sampler and the field’s nodes, so that a clean label can condition a noisy image.
  2. Block dropout and classifier-free guidance, so that the image score uses the label.
  3. Loss weights per block as an option of denoising_gradient (the example has its own).
  4. A class probability from a single root, and the unassigned mass passed on to coverage.
  5. A globalised solver (trust region, or continuation in the noise level) for modes in hundreds of free coordinates.
  6. Tolerances that follow the predictor’s precision (Float32 on a GPU stops near 1e-6).
  7. A convolutional energy network for image quality.

5. What the tutorial was meant to show

Classification accuracy and calibration; the class prototypes (modes) beside samples; an ambiguous digit with a two-component answer; inpainting of the lower half of a digit together with its label; and the cost per query against a diffusion classifier with ten denoising passes.

6. The experiment (October 2026)

Code: lib/VariationalDiffusion.jl/examples/mnist/ (its own environment; logs and models in ~/.cache/lenticulum/mnist/).

The model. An energy network E(z,t) on z=(x,y): the image x∈[−1,1]784, the label y one-hot, 16 time features. It is an MLP 810 → 1024 → 1024 → 1024 → 1 with swish activations, ε=σt​∇z​E, ad = SecondOrder(AutoForwardDiff(), AutoZygote()), and runs on an RTX 3080 through device. It is trained by denoising score matching with one noise level shared by image and label, AdamW with cosine decay, batch 256, 9 s per epoch. The label is 10 of 794 coordinates, and unweighted it barely enters the loss. Weighting its coordinates in the loss is what made the classifier work:

trainingclassification accuracy (1000 tuning images)
label weight 1, 40 epochs89.2%
label weight 20, 40 epochs93.4%
label weight 100, 100 epochs95.9%

Image → label. The query clamps the 784 pixels and frees the 10 label coordinates. The field’s noise levels were chosen on test images 9001–10000: 0.002–0.05, 0.05–0.2 and 0.1–0.4 gave 95.6%, 95.9% and 94.8%. Results on test images 1–9000:

classifieraccuracycost per query
root finding, one start, the label read as the argmax of the root96.1%18 ms (batches of 1000)
root finding from all ten class codes (implicit_classify)96.1%220 ms
the diffusion classifier of Li et al., same network, 32 shared noise draws × 10 classes95.8%3.1 ms (320 network passes)
a softmax MLP of the same width, 10 epochs97.7%

  • One answer per query. Every one of the 9000 queries has a single stable root: the ten starts all reach it. So implicit_mixture has one component, and its Laplace class probabilities are 0 or 1. The ten starts buy nothing, and the probabilities expected in §2 do not appear. The graded information is in where the root lies. A typical digit’s root is near its code (label coordinate about 0.8–1). For an atypical one it is between two codes:

    The “2” written like an “a” is at 2: 0.49 / 3: 0.48, the 6 at 6: 0.54 / 5: 0.54. A flat “0” is at 7: 0.18 / 6: 0.18: its root is far from every code. That is the unassigned case of categorical_answer (1.1% of the test set), and an answer coverage should flag.

  • Equal to the diffusion classifier, not cheaper. The two classifiers read the same network and agree in accuracy. The claim in §3 that a 10-dimensional solve beats ten Monte-Carlo estimates does not hold here. Each Newton iteration of the solve needs ten Hessian-vector products per noise level. The diffusion classifier’s 320 forward passes batch better on a GPU.

  • Both generative classifiers stay below the discriminative baseline (96 against 97.7%), as is usual for generative classifiers with small networks.

Label → image. The query clamps the label and frees the image. It fails, for two separate reasons.

  1. The samples are poor. An MLP over 794 joint coordinates gives noisy digits. With the energy parametrisation they are noisier than with an ε-network of the same size; a few are clean.

  2. The label hardly conditions the image. 20 samples per class were scored by a softmax MLP trained on real data (chance 10%):

    modelplainRePaint ×3Langevin ×2bothlabel held clean at every step
    ε-network11.5%10.0%12.5%12.5%13.0%
    energy, label weight 17.5%13.0%17.5%16.0%
    energy, label weight 10011.0%25.0%16.5%21.0%16.5%

    Holding the label clean instead of noising it does not help, so this is not the replacement method losing the label to noise. The network’s score for the image barely depends on the label. In the joint score, ∇x​logp(x,y)=∇x​logp(x)+∇x​logp(y∣x), the second term is small next to the image’s own structure, and nothing in the training makes the network resolve it. The label’s score does depend on the image, which is why classification works. The relation is learned well in one direction only.

Mode finding in the image (784 free, KrylovSolve) did not converge from a blank image or from a sample: the solve leaves the sample (1.7 to 6 per pixel, RMS) and ends unstable. The smoothed energy of this model has no usable basins in image space at the field’s noise levels. Inpainting with the label free was therefore not run.

What this says about the method. Classification as a query works at the accuracy of the established diffusion classifier, with a single generic solver, and its failure cases are visible: roots between the codes. Generation from the same relation needs what the text-to-image literature already found necessary:

  • classifier-free guidance, which needs training with the label dropped;
  • separate noise levels per block, as in UniDiffuser, so that a clean label can condition a noisy image;
  • a convolutional energy for image quality.

The first two are changes to the training and the sampler, not to the query machinery. The second needs a noise level per block of coordinates, which the package does not have.
