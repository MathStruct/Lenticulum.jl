Shared Solver Layer

Shared Solver Layer

23 Nov 202619 min read

  • design
  • implementation

design implementation

The design of P14: one package, lib/ImplicitSolvers.jl, holding what ImplicitLayers, VariationalDiffusion and MyceliumLearning’s graph adjoint each implement for themselves today: one tolerance rule, one solver report with a primal residual, the linear solves (dense and GMRES), the fixed-point and quasi-Newton root solvers, and the linear solve of the implicit-function adjoint. Element types come from the problem, never from a Float64 field. Newton and trust-region steps stay in VariationalDiffusion for now (phase 2). Status: design, 2026-11-22; W1 implemented the same day.

Sources: original to this vault (design). Walker & Ni, Anderson acceleration for fixed-point iterations, SIAM J. Numer. Anal. 49(4), 2011 (Anderson mixing; walker2011anderson); Saad & Schultz, GMRES, SIAM J. Sci. Stat. Comput. 7(3), 1986 (restarted GMRES; saad1986gmres); Bai, Kolter & Koltun, Deep Equilibrium Models, NeurIPS 2019 (the adjoint by the implicit function theorem; bai2019deq); Montoison & Orban, Krylov.jl: A Julia basket of hand-picked Krylov methods, JOSS 8(89), 2023 (the alternative; montoison2023krylov); code: lib/ImplicitLayers.jl/src/solve.jl, lib/VariationalDiffusion.jl/src/implicit.jl, krylov.jl, proxdm.jl, many.jl, lib/MyceliumLearning.jl/src/graph_adjoint.jl, lib/Mycelium.jl/src/passing.jl, bench/krylov_allocations.jl

Four report types, three GMRES (one hand-written, two through Krylov.jl), two fixed-point loops with opposite tolerance conventions, two adjoint linear solves of the same form. ImplicitLayers’ defaults are absolute and fixed for Float64: under Float32 the default 1e-10 is below rounding and never met; under dual numbers SolveReport cannot hold the residual.

Decisions

D1. A package of its own. lib/ImplicitSolvers.jl uses LinearAlgebra and has a ForwardDiff weak dependency. Random is used by tests. The consumers are ImplicitLayers, VariationalDiffusion and MyceliumLearning. Mycelium message passing keeps its own criterion (D2). The maintainer chose a standalone package on 2026-11-22.

D2. One tolerance rule. A solver has converged when

∥r∥≤atol+rtol⋅s,

with r the solver’s residual and s its scale, stated by each solver (∥u∥ for a root solver, ∥b∥ for a linear solve). This is Mycelium’s P9 criterion with one group, and VariationalDiffusion’s τ(1+∥z∥) is the case atol=rtol=τ.

  • Tolerance(; atol = nothing, rtol = nothing); the fields hold what was given (any Real, or nothing), and the comparison is made in the residual’s primal type.
  • A nothing resolves from the element type T of the problem: atol=rtol=τ(T)=max(10−9,30ϵ(T)), the rule VariationalDiffusion measured (implicit.md §11): 10−9 for Float64, 3.6⋅10−6 for Float32. A BigFloat gets 10−9 too; a caller that wants more passes it.
  • Guards against division by small numbers (the 1e-14 of the Broyden updates) are sqrt(eps(T)) times the scale of the quantity divided, not literals.
  • Mycelium’s propagate! keeps its keywords and per-group scales: it is not a root solver of a vector, and its defaults are covered by many tests. It shares the semantics, not the code.

D3. One report. SolverReport{R<:Real} with converged::Bool, iterations::Int, residual::R, nfe::Int and reason::Symbol (:converged, :maxiters, :nonfinite, :stalled). residual is always the primal value (ImplicitSolvers.primal, the identity, with a method for ForwardDiff.Dual in the extension), and the primal iteration stops on that value (Mycelium’s I23 rule, LP-053 Findings of the review of I23). After primal convergence GMRES and MINRES can add tangent corrections and count their work in the same report (P38, lib/ImplicitSolvers.jl/src/linsolve.md).

  • ImplicitLayers.SolveReport becomes const SolveReport = ImplicitSolvers.SolverReport (same field names, plus reason); its show moves along.
  • VariationalDiffusion.ImplicitSolution keeps its fields (z, residual, iters, converged, stable); residual becomes the primal value. QueryReport is a report about a query, not a solve, and stays.
  • Mycelium.ConvergenceReport stays (it has exact and a reason::String read by tests).

D4. Linear solves. linsolve(A, b, alg; tol, left, right) -> (x, report), with A a matrix or an operator action v↦Av. DenseSolve() uses LU or Cholesky for a matrix asserted to be symmetric positive definite. GMRES(; restart = 40, maxiters = 200) is matrix-free and generic in the element type. It comes from VariationalDiffusion’s restarted GMRES. MINRES(; maxiters = 200) uses short Lanczos recurrences for Hermitian systems, including indefinite ones, and checks the true residual. The scale is s=∥b∥. CG, Lanczos and the log-determinant stay in VariationalDiffusion, their only user.

  • W5 removes the Krylov.jl extensions of ImplicitLayers and MyceliumLearning. lib/ImplicitSolvers.jl/test/krylov_oracle.jl retains the direct test dependency and checks agreement on generated systems.
  • Why Krylov.jl was dropped, what the in-house solvers lack, and the open choice: “Why not Krylov.jl” below (P40).

D5. Root solvers. solve_root(F, u0, alg; tol = Tolerance(), maxiters) -> (u, report) for F(u)=0, u an AbstractVector. Algorithms: Picard(; damping = 1) (u←u−αF(u)), Broyden(; init_scale = 1), LimitedBroyden(; memory = 20) (with the history kept for the adjoint when asked, as ImplicitLayers’ BroydenIFT needs). These are ImplicitLayers’ solvers moved, with D2 and D3. ImplicitLayers’ constructor names (PicardSolver, BroydenSolver, LimitedBroydenSolver) stay as its own names for the moved types. A fixed point u=T(u) is the root of F(u)=u−T(u); VariationalDiffusion’s prox_infer uses Picard with its damping.

  • Newton with Armijo backtracking and the trust region are not in phase 1: implicit_infer checks stability, runs on devices and has a batched copy (many.jl); moving them is phase 2 (a row of its own), together with removing that copy.

D6. The adjoint linear solve. adjoint_solve(vjp, g, alg; tol, left, right) solves J∗λ=g given v↦J∗v (vjp). The same keywords on fixed_point_adjoint solve (I−J∗)λ=g through D4. Each package keeps the rules that know its parameters and cotangents. The shared solve and tolerance serve ImplicitLayers’ DenseIFT and KrylovIFT, VariationalDiffusion’s _adjoint_solve and _prox_adjoint, and MyceliumLearning’s dense and GMRES graph adjoints. JacobiIFT and BroydenIFT stay in ImplicitLayers.

D7. One finite-difference Jacobian. fd_jacobian(F, u; step = nothing), central differences, the step 3ϵ(T)​max(1,∣ui​∣) per coordinate. It replaces both copies; their users are tests and diagnostics.

What must not change

  • Every existing test of the consumers passes with its present tolerances, except where a deliberate change below says otherwise.
  • Exported names of ImplicitLayers and VariationalDiffusion keep working (aliases where the type moved).
  • Gradients through the rules: the same values (tolerance of the solves) on the existing tests.
  • No consumer’s load time grows by more than 10 % (@time using, measured before and after).

Deliberate changes

  • ImplicitLayers’ default tolerance: absolute 10−10 becomes D2’s default, 10−9(1+∥u∥) for Float64. A test that needs the old bound passes tol explicitly; each such test is listed in the work package’s report.
  • Reports hold the primal residual (D3).
  • prox_infer uses D2 to test the fixed-point residual at the returned iterate. The W3 baseline comparison measures the change against the old bound. The finite-difference fallback serves production prox adjoints and keeps its previous step.
  • Preconditioned GMRES decides convergence from the true residual against D2 with scale ∥b∥. It checks that residual after each cycle.
  • GMRES accepts complex systems using conjugating inner products and unitary Givens rotations.
  • MyceliumLearning’s :krylov is an alias of :gmres for existing callers.

Work packages

Each one is a branch, reviewed by a model that did not write it.

  1. W1, the package. Done 2026-11-22 (Codex; GLM’s review and its three fixes in LP-078). Departures: none of D2–D7; a root solve started exactly at the root returns the initial guess’s tangent under ForwardDiff, not the implicit derivative (three @test_broken; consumers’ rules supply the implicit derivative). D2–D7 with their tests, written from this note: the tolerance rule at its edges (zero residual, atol = 0, rtol = 0, both nothing for Float32, Float64, BigFloat, duals); linsolve dense against GMRES on random well-conditioned, nonsymmetric and symmetric positive definite systems (sizes 1, 2, 50), as functions and as matrices, Float32 and Float64; GMRES reports :maxiters on a system it cannot solve in the budget; root solvers on closed-form problems (a linear F, u−cosu componentwise, a contraction with a known fixed point), the iteration count unchanged under duals with the tangent of the root equal to the implicit-function theorem’s; the adjoint solve against (J')\g and (I - J')\g. Aqua. A CI entry and a docs page.
  2. W2, ImplicitLayers onto it. Done 2026-11-23 (Codex; reviewed by Mistral, LP-079). Root solvers, the report, finite differences, CPU dense vector solves and real unpreconditioned GMRES use the shared package; device LU, JacobiIFT and preconditioned, complex or ldiv Krylov solves keep their own paths. No test needed an explicit tol (every one was already explicit); three assertions changed to the shared API’s behaviour (LP-079). Its tests need Enzyme ≥ 0.13.221.
  3. W3, VariationalDiffusion onto it. Done 2026-11-23 (Codex; reviewed by Mistral, LP-079). Shared Picard, GMRES and adjoints implement D4–D6, and ImplicitSolution.residual is primal. The package suite passes; the eight VariationalDiffusion smoke examples printed the same numbers before and after (Codex’s run). D7’s step left all three diagnostic verdicts unchanged; the production fallback (prox_jacobian, used by the prox adjoints without an AD backend) kept its 1e-6 step. Newly refused: a negative or NaN tol, a negative maxiters, a nonfinite dense adjoint system (LP-079).
  4. W4, MyceliumLearning’s graph adjoint onto D6. Done 2026-11-23 (Codex). The dense reference solve and the new matrix-free :gmres use ImplicitSolvers. The graph retains its original residual check for all three solvers, including the Krylov.jl extension. Across the 18 graph-adjoint cases in scratch/w4/compare.jl, all 52 dense and 27 Krylov gradients are bitwise equal to their baselines. The new GMRES gradients differ from the old dense gradients by at most 2.019⋅10−10 absolute and 8.304⋅10−10 relative in the infinity norm.
  5. W5, Krylov.jl out. Done 2026-11-23 (Codex). In lib/ImplicitSolvers.jl/test/krylov_oracle.jl, both solvers passed the true residual and conditioning bounds on 648 generated real and complex systems. The one-step budget case reports nonconvergence in both. The two extensions are removed. In scratch/w5/compare.jl, real unpreconditioned KrylovIFT results and all 52 recorded graph :gmres gradients are bitwise equal to the baseline. The preconditioned, ldiv and complex IFT results meet the fixture tolerances. For the old graph :krylov path versus the new alias, the largest differences are 3.028⋅10−10 absolute and 1.246⋅10−9 relative. The full comparison and load times are in IMPL-p14-w5.md. Device checks use JLArrays. The Lenticulum suite and hardware GPU execution are outside this run.

W1 first; W2, W3 and W4 are independent after it; W5 last. Phase 2 (Newton, the trust region and many.jl’s copy) gets its own row when phase 1 is done.

Phase 2: Newton and the trust region (P39)

Design, 2026-11-23, after phase 1 was done; implemented the same day (Codex, reviewed by Mistral, LP-085). Deliberate change found by the review: a trial point with a non-finite residual counts as ratio 0 and shrinks the radius (before, the NaN ratio kept the radius and the same step was retried until maxiters); no recorded call reaches it. The problem named tuple has three optional members beyond D8 (trial_residual, residual_norms, round_steps), which keep the old evaluation order and rounding bitwise; many.jl’s trust-region scale callback also evaluates the batch Jacobian that stable and model read, which relies on the driver calling scale on all unfinished columns first in each iteration. The two loops exist twice because the batched copy evaluates all unfinished queries in one network call per noise level; the steps, the radii and the acceptance are per query and are the same code written again. Other Newton iterations in the repository (proxdm.jl’s modified Newton, Lenticulum/src/likelihood.jl 495, AlgebraicFactors/src/fit.jl 36) are not in this phase (open questions).

D8. One driver, over columns. ImplicitSolvers gets the two iterations as algorithms over a batch of queries, a matrix U whose columns are the free coordinates of the queries; one query is a batch of one column. The relation supplies the evaluations through callbacks on the unfinished columns (cols):

  • residual(U, cols) -> R (the residual of each column, the root to find);
  • scale(U, cols) -> s (D2’s scale per column; VariationalDiffusion passes ∥z∥ of the whole state, clamps included);
  • for NewtonDescent: system(U, cols) -> (stable, solve), a Bool and a function r↦J−1r per column;
  • for TrustRegion: merit(U, R, cols) -> f and model(U, R, cols) -> (g, H) per column, H a symmetric matrix (the exact step) or a function v↦Hv (Steihaug); stable(U, cols) for the report.

solve_columns(problem, U0, alg; tol::Tolerance, maxiters) -> (U, reports::Vector{SolverReport}, stable::Vector{Bool}). A column leaves the batch when it converges or stops; the callbacks see only the unfinished ones, as many.jl does today. The driver is generic in the array type (no scalar indexing; a device matrix stays on the device, the per-column steps work on what the callbacks return).

D9. The algorithms. NewtonDescent(; step = 0.05, armijo = 1e-4, min_damping = 1e-8): today’s iteration, Newton with backtracking on ∥r∥ where stable, −step⋅r otherwise. TrustRegion(; radius = nothing, cg_maxiters = 200, cg_rtol = 1e-8, precision = nothing): today’s Algorithm 4.1 with its constants (shrink below ratio 1/4, grow above 3/4 at the boundary, accept above 10−4), the noise rule (a predicted decrease below 100ϵa​max(∣f∣,1) is decided by the residual) and the radius floor 10ϵa​(1+s), with ϵa​ = precision (the arithmetic’s epsilon, _arith_eps in the caller; eps of the element type when nothing). radius = nothing is 0.1max(nF​,1)​. The step routines _tr_step (both) and _to_boundary move with them. The relation decides which merit it has (its energy, or 21​∥r∥2 with the Gauss–Newton model): that stays in VariationalDiffusion’s model callback.

D10. D2 and D3 for these solves. VariationalDiffusion’s tol::Real keyword stays and becomes Tolerance(atol = tol, rtol = tol) with the scale above, so ∥r∥≤τ(1+∥z∥) is computed as τ+τ∥z∥ (a deliberate change at the last bit). ImplicitSolution keeps its fields, filled from the report (iters = iterations) and stable; continuation stays in VariationalDiffusion, around the driver.

D11. What stays in VariationalDiffusion. The field, its Jacobians and operators, the energies, the merit choice, _stable and _lambda_min, default_tolerance, continuation, implicit_classify, the pullback. implicit_infer and implicit_infer_many keep their signatures and results; they build the callbacks (one column, with prior_jacobian or the matrix-free operators; many columns, with _jacobian_many, _field_many, _energy_many) and call the driver. The two copies in many.jl go.

Must not change (phase 2). Each test of VariationalDiffusion passes with its tolerances; on every call of implicit_infer and implicit_infer_many in its suite and examples, z, iters, converged and stable equal the values before the change bitwise, except where the rounding of D10 flips a comparison (each such case listed with its margin). The trust-region and Newton steps of one query and of the same query in a batch keep the relation of today (they use different Jacobian routes and are not bitwise equal; the tests that compare them stay as they are). Load time of VariationalDiffusion within 10 %; bench/minres_energy.jl and examples/gpu_energy.jl (smoke) not slower beyond noise.

Why not Krylov.jl (record, 2026-11-24)

Written after the fact, at the maintainer’s request: P14 replaced Krylov.jl without anyone recording why or weighing the two options, and the result has gaps that Krylov.jl does not have. Open as P40.

The reason given. The W5 brief and D4 give only consolidation: “one solver, one tolerance rule, one report”. At the time Krylov.jl was used only by two optional extensions (KrylovIFT with a preconditioner, ldiv or a complex right-hand side; MyceliumLearning’s :krylov), and VariationalDiffusion already had its own restarted GMRES. W5 extended that GMRES and removed the extensions. Nobody checked whether Krylov.jl, wrapped, could have met D2 and D3. P37 then wrote MINRES in-house after measuring with Krylov.jl’s minres as a stand-in (LP-083).

What the in-house solvers have. The four points below are what they do, not reasons anyone gave before W5:

  • convergence on the true residual. In the 2×2 fixtures of lib/ImplicitSolvers.jl/test/krylov_oracle.jl with a nonuniform left Jacobi preconditioner, Krylov.jl stopped on its preconditioned estimate above the D2 bound (LP-082);
  • the D2 rule, with defaults from the problem’s element type, in place of Float64 fields;
  • under ForwardDiff, stopping on primal values and correcting tangents afterwards (P38, LP-084), including complex dual numbers (LP-082). The tangent correction runs after a converged solve and calls linsolve again. It does not depend on which Krylov code produced the primal solution;
  • a SolverReport with nfe.

Whether Krylov.jl’s callback and its handling of dual element types could give the same was not checked.

What they lack (the maintainer’s review, 2026-11-24, checked against linsolve.jl at ce4b0282):

  • Allocation-free use. linsolve has no in-place form and no workspace. On each call, GMRES allocates x0, r and the product ax. On each Arnoldi step it allocates the operator’s result, its copy and the normalised basis vector, pushed onto a growing Vector. On each cycle it allocates H, c, s and g, and on each restart xnew. The operator contract is v↦Av returning a new vector, not mul!(y, A, v). Measured with bench/krylov_allocations.jl (Julia 1.13.1, one thread, dense A=I+randn/(4n​), restart 40, tolerance 10−10, 17 iterations): the shared GMRES allocates 112 kB at n=200 and 948 kB at n=2000, about 70 and 59 vectors. Krylov.jl’s gmres! with a GmresWorkspace allocates 0 bytes. Its time is 0.057 against 0.073 ms at n=200, and 1.81 against 2.14 ms at n=2000. Time is close at these sizes; the allocations matter for repeated solves (every implicit adjoint, every Newton step) and on devices.
  • Types for dispatch. GMRES and MINRES hold only Int fields. Nothing is parametrised by the element type or the storage type, unlike a Krylov.jl workspace ({T, FC, S}). The operator is told apart only as AbstractMatrix versus anything else, and genericity is by duck typing on broadcasts.
  • Array types and GPUs. The device tests (lib/ImplicitSolvers.jl/test/device.jl) are JLArrays on 2×2 systems, which catches scalar indexing and nothing else. No CUDA or other hardware GPU run, no device size, and no other array types (views, ComponentArrays, distributed arrays) are tested. GMRES keeps H and g on the host and calls dot twice per basis vector per step, one device synchronisation each; this was not measured. The open question below (no device benchmark planned) is still open.
  • Preconditioners. GMRES takes left and right only as functions v↦Mv. There is no ldiv! object protocol, and no in-place form. The package provides no preconditioner itself: Jacobi appears only in tests. MINRES takes none. CG and Lanczos are a third copy in lib/VariationalDiffusion.jl/src/krylov.jl (_cg, _lanczos), with their own rtol.

Krylov.jl provides the missing pieces according to its documentation (Montoison and Orban 2023): in-place methods on preallocated workspaces, mul! operators, the preconditioners M and N including ldiv! objects, and GPU arrays. None of these was tried here.

The open choice (P40). (a) Krylov.jl as the backend of linsolve for GMRES, MINRES and CG. Tolerance, SolverReport, a final true-residual check and the tangent correction remain a wrapper. The oracle test then tests the wrapper against DenseSolve. (b) Keep the in-house solvers and add what they lack: workspaces, mul! operators, ldiv! preconditioners, and a hardware GPU test and benchmark.

Before (a), check three things: Krylov.jl on ForwardDiff.Dual and complex Dual element types, on JLArrays with allowscalar(false), and whether its callback can stop on the true residual under left preconditioning. (b) is the larger amount of code to own.

Open questions

  • Whether Anderson belongs in the package as a root solver (Mycelium’s AndersonSchedule mixes messages, not a vector; nothing else uses Anderson today). Not in phase 1.
  • A device (GPU) path for GMRES: the moved code must not scalar-index (tested with JLArrays and allowscalar(false)), but no device benchmark is planned.
