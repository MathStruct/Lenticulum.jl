Composing Stochastic Factors

Composing Stochastic Factors

01 Nov 202614 min read

  • design
  • overview

design overview

What is the smallest stochastic factor from which an expressive generative model can be built by composition alone, with no neural network inside any factor? Ten experiments on the branch experiment-qm answer it in four parts:

  • a linear constraint whose right-hand side is chosen by a discrete outcome (the switching constraint factor) is the building block;
  • composed, these factors give exactly the finite Gaussian mixtures, and composition pays only when the wiring matches the data;
  • a chain of them trained as a diffusion is held back by its objective, not by its model class;
  • learning through the graph needs exact blocks of switches and mean-field iteration between them.

The factor and the message rule are now in the package. The experiments stay on the branch.

1. The question

The project’s implicit learners put a network inside a factor (a denoiser, an equilibrium layer). This line of work asked the opposite question: keep every factor mathematically simple, linear or nearly so, and let the factor graph carry the complexity. A factor should sample, support several polarities (any variable may be the one inferred), be a statistical game with a free energy (Scalar and Multivariate Energy), learn by gradients, and compose under message passing and the Bethe free energy.

The starting idea was quantum-flavoured: a linear factor whose output is an eigenvector of a learned density matrix, drawn by its eigenvalue, as a one-shot alternative to iterative denoising.

2. Theory

The building block

Linear-Gaussian factors never produce a second mode, however they are wired (Switching Constraint Factors, Proposition 1). The smallest addition that does, while keeping every message, every factor belief and every local free energy in closed form, is a discrete outcome selecting the right-hand side of a linear constraint. A closure lemma makes “smallest” exact: a factor with a discrete channel keeps messages in the finite conditional-Gaussian family exactly when each outcome’s potential is a sum of Gaussian sites. The resulting switching constraint factor wk​N(∑i​Ai​xi​−bk​;0,Q) has:

  • a measurement-like message to the outcome, the evidence of each outcome;
  • a mixture message to every continuous channel, with the per-outcome constants kept;
  • an exact factor entropy, H(k)+H(x∣k).

It is the conditional-Gaussian model of Lauritzen & Wermuth, made into a factor with polarities.

The quantum reading, done properly

The spectral construction has no joint distribution behind it. Its outcome weights treat a partial input as a state, so its polarities are not compatible (inverting one query does not give another), and its modes are forced to be orthogonal. Made into a joint, a measurement is a Born joint p(k,z)∝N(z)z~⊤Ek​z~ with a POVM {Ek​}. Three things were proved about it:

  • Its marginal does not depend on the POVM: p(z)∝N(z)∥z~∥2. With weighted outcomes it becomes one quadratic tilt N(z)z~⊤Mz~.
  • What it adds is in queries about the outcome: p(z∣k) can vanish exactly on a hyperplane, which no finite mixture can.
  • Density needs amplitudes: squared polynomials times a Gaussian are dense in L1, but that needs amplitudes added before squaring, as in a Born machine. A factor graph multiplies probabilities. Its products of positive semidefinite quadratics are dense in one dimension, but cannot vanish on a circle in two.

So interference, the genuinely quantum ingredient, needs amplitude-level composition, which a probabilistic factor graph does not have. The switching factor is the building block; the Born joint is a possible later belief type for outcome queries.

Composition

A graph of switching factors with L outcomes, linear-Gaussian factors and Gaussian priors has a finite Gaussian mixture as its joint, with at most ∏l​Kl​ components (Switching Constraint Factors, Proposition 4). Composition buys parameter efficiency, not a larger class. L binary switches feeding a sum node, z=c+∑l​kl​δl​+ε, give a 2L-component grid from O(L) parameters: a factorial model (Ghahramani & Jordan 1997), here one time slice. A switch over such sums is a small sum-product network (Poon & Domingos 2011).

The diffusion reading

A diffusion is a chain of reverse kernels. Three results place the switching factor in it:

  • For mixture data the exact reverse kernels are switching kernels. If the data are a K-component Gaussian mixture, every true reverse conditional of the variance-preserving forward process is a switching linear kernel with K outcomes. Its gate is the measurement of the factor, and its parameters follow from the noise schedule, so a shared, time-conditioned parameterisation comes for free (proved on the branch, design note Proposition 8).
  • The bound decouples. With a fixed forward process q, the evidence bound of any reverse chain p is Elogq0​−KL(qT​∥pT​)−∑t​Eqt​​KL(q(⋅∣zt​)∥pt​(⋅∣zt​)): the unconditioned variant of Sohl-Dickstein et al. (2015, App. B.4) and Ho et al. (2020, §2). Maximising it fits each step to the data’s own noised reverse conditional, independently. It rewards a chain for following the forward process’s marginals, not for reaching q0​ some other way.
  • The class is larger than the bound lets it be. A chain of L two-outcome kernels with constant gates and offset steps has exactly the additive model’s 2L-component marginal (design note Proposition 10). So chains of few-outcome kernels can represent product grids, and any limit found under the bound is the objective’s, not the class’s.

  • Composition is a prior on how components are arranged. It pays when the prior is right, and then most at small sample sizes.
  • Wiring matters. Constraints on one variable intersect: with more switches than dimensions some configurations cannot be satisfied. With as many switches as dimensions (a 4-mode grid, L=n=2) they match the additive model (0.004 against 0.005).
  • Exact EM trains these models. The M-step is closed form, a weighted least squares. 256 EM starts are 4–5 times faster than 16 Adam starts at the same optima.
  • Greedy structure search works on these data. Four moves (add or remove a switch, split or merge a cluster), scored by BIC under exact EM, recover the generating structure on the 8-mode grid and a tree, and find a more compact exact structure on the ring. Every greedy model is within 0.005 nats of the truth.

The diffusion reading: E4(a), the small-step test, E4(b), the objective control

  • E4(a) (switching_reverse_chain_e4a.md): on a three-component mixture, the exact kernels make every path’s bound equal logq0​ to 1.6⋅10−14. The shared, time-conditioned chain (17 parameters) reaches −2.123 against the truth’s −2.108. Linear-Gaussian steps fail at any step size (−3.58).
  • Small steps (switching_small_steps.md): with each step fitted on its own, three outcomes per step (as many as components) match the truth up to the estimation cost of fitting T kernels. Two outcomes keep a gap of 0.50–0.68 nats however small the steps. Small steps make each reverse conditional unimodal, but its mean, the score field, still needs one linear piece per component.
  • E4(b) (switching_chain_e4b.md): on two moons and a ring, which no finite mixture contains, a chain of T≤20 steps with K outcomes each scores like a flat K-component mixture (within 0.006 nats at T=5). The best chain is 0.31–0.34 nats from the truth with up to 1 085 parameters; a flat 16-mixture is within 0.02 with 95.
  • The objective control (chain_objective.md): on a 16-mode grid, the additive model rewritten as a 4-step chain has the same marginal (7⋅10−15). Within one family (constant-gate chains, scored exactly), the bound’s optimum is 0.93–1.16 nats from the truth, while exact likelihood finds a member 0.001 away. Only the objective differs.
  • A neural reference (neural_reference.md): the standard Gaussian reverse kernel of Ho et al., with an MLP noise predictor, trained by the same bound on the same data. At T=1000 it reaches the ring (0.009 nats, better than any mixture) but not moons (0.315, like a 4-outcome chain). At T=20 it is worse than the switching chains.

So under the diffusion bound with a fixed forward process, depth in time does not substitute for outcomes per step. The bound trains a chain well only when each step’s kernel can follow the true reverse kernel; K-outcome, linearly gated steps cannot.

Inference in the graph: E6, E7 and the hybrid

The additive wiring in 8 dimensions, with 4 to 12 overlapping switches and five random truths. Every E-step feeds the same closed-form M-step, started at the true parameters (factorial_inference.md, hierarchy_inference.md, switching_blocks_graph.md):

The rows say three things:

  • The bias is in the pair moments. With exact marginals and factorised pair moments, EM is nearly as far off as with the approximate marginals. At the truth the M-step misses the explaining-away covariance, so the truth is not a fixed point (a known hazard of factorised variational EM; see Turner & Sahani 2011). Linear-response pair moments (Welling & Teh 2004) made it worse; EP’s joint Gaussian helps at L=8 but is unreliable at L=12 and three to four orders of magnitude slower.
  • It takes exact blocks and iteration. Blocks of L/2 switches chosen by overlap, each a switching factor with 2L/2 outcomes, halve the bias in one pass. Iterating with the other blocks’ current means halves it again. Blocks that ignore which switches overlap help much less, and iteration without blocks (mean field) does not help at all.
  • Mycelium now does it. With the sum node on MeanField() and the blocks on belief propagation, the graph is the hybrid of Riegler et al. (2013). It reproduces the structured mean field to 9⋅10−7 per point and 3⋅10−8 in fitted parameters (L-ADR-0009 Factor nodes carry a message rule).

The same holds one level up (E7, a switch over sums): mean field over the switches inside a cluster is 0.25–0.68 nats off, overlap blocks 0.03–0.33.

4. What it adds up to

  1. The building block exists and is in the package. SwitchingConstraintFactor is closed form in every polarity, passes the factor conformance checks, and reduces to LinearConstraintFactor with one outcome.
  2. Expressivity comes from wiring, not depth. The class of every composition is the finite mixtures; what composition changes is how many parameters a given arrangement of components costs. A sum node fed by switches is the wiring that pays.
  3. A diffusion of switching steps is limited by its objective. The fixed-forward bound projects every step onto K linear pieces. Exact marginal likelihood trains the same chains to the truth, but needs exact switch posteriors.
  4. Learning through the graph is an inference problem. Factorised beliefs bias the parameters through the pair moments. Exact blocks plus mean-field iteration remove most of the bias, and that combination is now expressible in Mycelium with a per-node message rule.
  5. The quantum route needs amplitudes. Born measurements, read as joints, add outcome queries and exact zeros, but not density: interference lives at the amplitude level, which probabilistic factor graphs do not compose.

5. Open

  • The general form of the factor (per-outcome Ak​, Qk​), needed for non-parallel modes, and the directed switching kernel for chains.
  • A free energy for graphs with mean-field nodes, so that parameter_step can train them, and entropies of mixture beliefs at variables (Extending Mycelium).
  • How to choose blocks of switches at scale, and whether blocks still help where exact inference is infeasible (L≳15, untested).
  • A training objective for compositional chains that scales: exact likelihood works but needs exact posteriors; a learned forward process is untested.
  • Whether positive sums of products of positive semidefinite quadratics times a Gaussian are dense in L1(Rn) for n≥2.

6. How the work was done

The design note was written first, with each claim marked as proved, known or expected, and then reviewed twice by four language models (ChatGPT, Gemini, Mistral, Grok). Every review claim was checked before it was accepted. The reviews corrected:

  • a wrong inversion formula;
  • a parameter count (15K−6 identified parameters per kernel, not 15K−1);
  • an invalid estimation-cost subtraction;
  • the name of the graph E-step (assumed-density filtering, not belief propagation).

They also prompted the controls above: five seeds, EP, the objective control and the neural reference. Both rounds are recorded in §13 of the branch’s design note. The experiments stay on the branch: they are evidence, not interface.
