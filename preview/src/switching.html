switching

03 Nov 202616 min read

  • implementation

implementation

SwitchingConstraintFactor: a linear constraint whose right-hand side is chosen by one discrete outcome, 0=∑i​Ai​xi​−bk​+ε with P(k)=wk​. It is the smallest factor that leaves the Gaussian class, and every message and the local free energy are closed form. The theory is in Switching Constraint Factors; the experiments that led to it (E1–E7, on the branch experiment-qm) are summarised in Composing Stochastic Factors.

Sources: Riegler, Kirkelund, Manchon, Badiu & Fleury, IEEE Trans. Inf. Theory 59(1), 2013 (the hybrid BP/mean-field scheme of §4b). code: switching.jl, constraint.jl, gaussian.jl; tests: test/switching_constraint.jl; experiment: examples/switching_blocks_graph.jl (branch experiment-qm)

Bibliography: Riegler et al. 2013

Theory (CT-ML wiki): Bayesian Inversion · Open Model · Variational Free Energy

1. The factor

f(x1​,…,xn​,k)=wk​N(i∑​Ai​xi​−bk​; 0, Q),k∈{1,…,K}.

The parameters are (A, b, logw): the coefficients A (a NamedTuple keyed by channel), the m × K matrix b of right-hand sides and the unnormalised log-weights, w=softmax(logw). Q is a fixed hyperparameter, as in LinearConstraintFactor. The outcome is a channel of its own (switch, default :k) with K states.

A and Q are shared by the outcomes, the “location-shift” case of §11 of the design note. That is what keeps the factor small: every outcome induces a Gaussian with the same precision, so the outcomes differ only in a mean.

2. Messages

With dk​=bk​−∑i=t​Ai​mi​ and R=Q+∑i=t​Ai​Si​Ai⊤​ for incoming N(mi​,Si​):

  • to the outcome: P(k)∝wk​N(dk​;0,R), a CategoricalBelief;
  • to a continuous channel t, with πk​ the incoming outcome belief (flat if latent or absent):
μc→t​(xt​)∝k∑​πk​wk​N(At​xt​;dk​,R),

a MixtureBelief of the canonical Gaussians N−1(At⊤​R−1dk​, At⊤​R−1At​). An observed outcome (a DiracBelief with an index) gives one GaussianBelief, the LinearConstraintFactor message with c=bk​.

The per-outcome constants go into the mixture weights, and how depends on whether the components are proper:

  • if At​ has full column rank, the components are normalised Gaussians and the weight of outcome k is logπk​wk​−21​dk⊤​R−1dk​+21​ηk⊤​Λ−1ηk​;
  • otherwise the components are improper, and Mycelium’s mixture product (messages.jl, _mixture_product) treats each as the unnormalised exponential exp(η⊤x−21​x⊤Λx), so the weight is logπk​wk​−21​dk⊤​R−1dk​.

The test pools both kinds with a Gaussian prior and compares the result, weights and components, with plain Gaussian conditioning outcome by outcome.

3. The local free energy

The factor’s belief bc​(x,k)∝f(x,k)μk​(k)∏i​μi​(xi​) is, for each outcome, a Gaussian over the free channels, and all of them have the same precision Λ=G⊤Q−1G+blockdiag(Λi​). So its entropy is closed form:

H(bc​)=H(q)+H(x∣k),qk​∝μk​(k)wk​exp(−21​ck⊤​Q−1ck​+21​ηk⊤​Λ−1ηk​),

with ck​=bk​ minus the clamped channels’ contributions and ηk​=G⊤Q−1ck​+∑i​ηi​. The local free energy Fc​=Ebc​​[−logf]−H(bc​) is graded as (fit, complexity, negentropy):

partvalue
fit∑k​qk​21​(rˉk⊤​Q−1rˉk​+trQ−1Covr)
complexity21​logdet2πQ−∑k​qk​logwk​
negentropy−H(q)−H(x∣k)

The tests check it three ways: against LinearConstraintFactor at K=1 (all three parts); against the identity Fc​=Ebc​​[logπ+logρ]−logZ for a Gaussian message π on a latent channel and a categorical one ρ on the outcome; and, in a Mycelium graph (outcome prior, factor, clamped data), the Bethe free energy equals −logp(z) of the Gaussian mixture.

4. Polarities and the game interface

Any assignment with one channel unobserved, the outcome included, and the others observed or latent: supported_polarities lists all n⋅2n−1 of them, the set supports_polarity accepts (L-SPEC-001), in any key order. A latent outcome is marginalised under the factor’s own weights; a latent continuous channel is integrated out under a flat prior (§5). Both are graph variables summed or integrated over by the message, not a latent space of the open model. The open model is pure and its inversion exact, as for LinearConstraintModel. The pointwise energy is −logf(x,k), a scalar. The factor passes check_factor_conformance in every polarity.

4b. Mean-field messages

On a MeanField() node (Mycelium passing.md, “Mean-field nodes”) the factor sends expEq​[logf] over the other channels’ current marginals. For each outcome logf is quadratic in the continuous channels, so only means enter. With sˉ=∑i=t​Ai​Eq​[xi​] and q the outcome’s marginal, the message to a continuous channel is N−1(At⊤​Q−1(bˉ−sˉ),At⊤​Q−1At​) with bˉ=∑k​qk​bk​. The message to the outcome is P(k)∝wk​exp(−21​(bk​−sˉ)⊤Q−1(bk​−sˉ)). The test checks both against the formulas, and the slope and curvature of Eq​[logf] against Monte Carlo. LinearConstraintFactor has the same rule with one outcome (constraint.jl).

Mean-field energy (2026-11-02). The node’s term in the hybrid free energy is ∑k​qk​(21​E∥rk​∥Q−12​−logwk​) plus the normaliser, under independent marginals on the continuous channels and the outcome (q from a categorical or point marginal), with no entropy (L-SPEC-005 The Hybrid Free Energy). It reuses belief_free_energy with the product belief (one mean column per outcome) and drops the negentropy, which would otherwise include H(q). Checked against Monte Carlo in test/mean_field_free_energy.jl.

5. What it does not do

  • Per-outcome coefficients or noise. With Ak​ or Qk​ depending on the outcome, the outcome Gaussians have different precisions: the message to a continuous channel is still a mixture, but the components no longer share a precision, and improper components with different precisions cannot be weighted consistently. Experiment E1’s two crossing lines need this (different Ak​). Not implemented.
  • Mixture inputs are collapsed to their moments before use, in the messages and in the free energy, as for LinearConstraintFactor (gaussian.md, “Mixture inputs”). So a chain of switching factors on belief propagation runs as assumed-density filtering, and experiment E6 measured the bias that causes when switches overlap. Putting the sum node on MeanField() removes the collapse to prior moments: it then reads the other inputs’ current means (Composing Stochastic Factors, E7). Since 2026-11-03 a node on MixtureReduction(M) expands mixture inputs instead, exactly, and reduces its outgoing message to M components (§9).
  • Bethe free energy with mixture marginals. Mycelium has no entropy for a Gaussian-mixture variable belief, so a graph’s Bethe free energy is available only when every continuous variable whose marginal is a mixture has degree one, or is clamped. The factor’s own entropy is exact.
  • The directed variant of §8 of the design note (a switching kernel normalised by Zt​(zt​), with gates that depend on the state) is a different factor and is not here.
  • Flat and improper inputs are handled, exactly (the bullet is kept for the history). An uninformative continuous input (no message, TrivialBelief(), Latent()) is integrated out under a flat prior: R−1 becomes the projection that removes the range of those channels’ A‘s (constraint.md §4.1, the same helper). Only the part of dk​ they cannot reach then separates the outcomes. Until 2026-11-01 the factor returned TrivialBelief() here unless that channel’s A had full row rank, and it looked only at the first such channel. Since 2026-11-02 a Gaussian input that is improper in some directions only is split, too: its proper part enters dk​ and R, its null space joins the flat directions.

6. Difficulties met

  • Rank-deficient precisions round to positive definite. With one equation and a 2-D target, At⊤​R−1At​ has rank one but passes isposdef after rounding often enough. Mycelium then treats the components as normalised Gaussians of enormous variance and weights them by 21​η⊤Λ−1η, which is rounding noise. The test caught it: the pooled weights were off by an order of magnitude. Until 2026-11-02 the factor zeroed the null-space eigenvalues of a rank-deficient precision and pushed them below zero, starting at −ϵλmax​, until the result no longer factorised. That made the precision indefinite at rounding level, which a 2026-11-01 review flagged. Now the factor decides properness by the rank of the projected At​ and declares it (GaussianBelief(η, Λ, true), LenticulumCore gaussian_belief.md, “Declared improper”); Λ=(TAt​)⊤(TAt​) is passed as computed, a Gram matrix, with no repair. The mean-field message and LinearConstraintFactor’s message with flat inputs declare it the same way. The review’s case, a proper neighbour with precision below ϵλmax​ in a null direction, turned out to need a condition number above 1/ϵ in the pooled precision: no dense Float64 representation holds that, flag or no flag. The test (“rank-deficient components are declared improper”) pools with a prior of condition number about 2⋅109 and matches Gaussian conditioning outcome by outcome.

7. The parameter gradient holds the belief fixed (LP-020, 2026-11-01)

Mycelium.factor_belief freezes the whole belief bc​(x,k): the outcome posterior q, one mean per outcome, and the shared covariance. belief_free_energy re-evaluates ∑k​qk​(21​E∥rk​∥Q−12​−logwk​)+21​logdet2πQ at the current A, b and log-weights, with the entropy H(q)+H(x∣k) constant. So the log-weights’ gradient is −(q−w), the EM M-step direction, and A and b get theirs at fixed responsibilities. This matches finite differences of the converged Bethe free energy in test/lp020.jl. Construction: gaussian.md, “The factor belief, held fixed”.

9. Mixture inputs expanded (2026-11-03)

Mycelium.mixture_message(f, target, ...) is the exact message when inputs are Gaussian mixtures, for MixtureReduction(M) nodes (Extending Mycelium §4, L-SPEC-008 Expectation Propagation and Mixture Reduction). Each mixture input ∑j​vij​N(mij​,Sij​) is a hidden index; a combination c of components gives Rc​=Q+∑i=t​Ai​Sici​​Ai⊤​ and dkc​=bk​−∑i=t​Ai​mici​​, and carries the evidence ∏i​vici​​N(dkc​;0,Rc​). Unlike the components of factor_message, these differ in Rc​, so the weight keeps −21​logdetRc​, and a proper component, normalised, also 21​η⊤Λc−1​η−21​logdetΛc​+2d​log2π. Improper components stay unnormalised exponentials, as in factor_message.

Flat directions of the non-mixture inputs are integrated out as there (_residual_metric), and the weight then needs the finite part of the diverging normaliser. For a prior N(0,τΣ0​) along the flat coefficients G, logdet(R+τGΣ0​G⊤)=logdetR+ρlogτ+2∑i≤ρ​logsi​+logdet(Vρ⊤​Σ0​Vρ​)+o(1), with si​ and Vρ​ the nonzero singular values and right singular vectors of L−1G (R=LL⊤). The span of Vρ​ is the row space of G whatever L is, so the last term is the same for every combination, and _residual_metric_logdet returns logdetRc​+2∑i​logsi​. Mixture components themselves must be proper; a flat direction inside one would change G between combinations.

LinearConstraintFactor is the case K=1, b1​=c (constraint.jl calls the same kernel). Tests (test/mixture_rules.jl): log-density differences of the message against the pointwise sum over components and outcomes to 10−12, to a continuous channel and to the outcome, for both factors; with a flat input beside the mixture, against a prior of variance 108 to 10−6; and equal to factor_message without mixture inputs. On a switching linear chain the reduction budget then interpolates between assumed-density filtering and the exact posterior (passing.md).

10. Plates of switching factors (2026-11-03)

Two batched methods for a plate of tied switching factors (L-ADR-0010, blocks.md):

  • factor_messages on blocks, to the outcome, when the continuous inputs are points, trivial, or proper Gaussians with one shared precision: one metric T, then logPi​(k)=logwk​−21​∥T(bk​−si​)∥2 normalised per column, as a CategoricalBlock. The squared norm is taken of the difference, not expanded into ∥Tbk​∥2−2(Tbk​)⊤Tsi​+∥Tsi​∥2, which would cancel for data far from the origin. To a continuous channel the message is a mixture and goes replica by replica.
  • factor_beliefs, belief_free_energies and local_free_energies through FrozenSwitchingBeliefs: with the continuous messages as point blocks or shared-precision Gaussian blocks and the outcome’s as a categorical or trivial block, the belief precision, covariance and H(x∣k) are computed once; per replica the means of every outcome and the posterior qi​, as switch_statistics computes them one at a time. belief_free_energies is written without mutation for Zygote.

test/plates.jl (“Plates of switching factors on message blocks”) checks both against the same messages per edge: a mixture over observed points (40 replicas), and a latent mixture on expectation-propagation nodes (the outcome and z latent, x=z+δ observed): messages to the sequential schedule’s within 10−10, the free energy to 10−12 relative, each graded term to 10−12, the gradient to 10−10 with ForwardDiff and Zygote and 10−6 with reference differences. Timings (bench/mixture_plates.jl, blocks.md): at N=1000 the E-step takes 0.10 ms on blocks against 9.7 ms edge by edge and 0.044 ms by hand, F 1.1 ms against 5.1 ms, ∇F 2.9 ms against 29 ms.

The first version folded the point channels with foldl(...; init); Zygote’s pullback of that fails on an empty list (a plate whose continuous channels are all latent), so it is a reduce behind an emptiness check.

12. Mixture messages as one block (2026-11-03)

_switch_mixture_block, called by the block method of factor_messages for a continuous target: with the other continuous inputs as for the outcome method (points, trivial, or proper Gaussians of one precision), the outcome’s input trivial, absent or a CategoricalBlock, and K>1, the messages of all replicas are one MixtureBlock: one metric T and one precision (TAt​)⊤(TAt​), ηki​=(TAt​)⊤T(bk​−si​), and the weights of _switch_message (logπki​+logwk​−21​∥T(bk​−si​)∥2, plus 21​η⊤Λ−1η for proper components). An outcome impossible in some replica sends the group to the per-replica path, since _switch_message drops it and the replicas would differ in K; a target the factor cannot reach gives a TrivialBlock. Tested in test/plates.jl (“Mixture blocks”) against the sequential schedule; timings in blocks.md.

13. Cluster forms (2026-11-03)

Mycelium.cluster_form and cluster_switch for SwitchingConstraintFactor (the residual ∑i​Ai​xi​−bk​, noise Q, the normalised log-weights, the switch channel), LinearConstraintFactor (K=1, b1​=c) and GaussianPrior without learnable noise (x−μ, noise Σ) are defined here, so these factors can be put in a cluster node (lib/Mycelium.jl/src/cluster.md, L-SPEC-009 Cluster Nodes). Three switches in one cluster send the messages of the 8-outcome block factor that E7 built by hand (test/cluster.jl).
