Energies and Entropies

24 Oct 20269 min read

  • definition

definition overview

The vault and the code use “energy” for at least seven different objects and “entropy” for five, and they are easy to confuse. This note separates them. The first table is the whole point; every later section explains one row, says where it lives in the code, and how it relates to its neighbours. Read it before Scalar and Multivariate Energy, Bethe Free Energy or Energy-Based Learning.

2. Energies on points

Pointwise energy (row 1). What a factor charges at one configuration, before any scalarisation: for the Gaussian, constraint and nonlinear factors the residual, a signed vector that is zero exactly on the relation; for a likelihood factor the negative log-likelihood as a one-element vector; for a diffusion factor a graded pair (clamp, score). It is kept a vector because its Jacobian is what Gauss–Newton steps, the Fisher metric and the implicit function theorem need (Scalar and Multivariate Energy §6; L-ADR-0002 Energies are vectors, scalarised late).

Scalar pointwise energy (row 2). The pointwise energy made a number by a scalarisation, e.g. 21​r⊤Q−1r with SquaredNorm(Q⁻¹). It is zero at a perfect fit by design (Scalar and Multivariate Energy §8, “Normalised or not”).

Negative log-likelihood (row 3). Row 2 plus the normaliser 21​logdet(2πQ). The two differ by a constant while Q is fixed and are different objectives once Q is learned: row 2 alone drives Q→0 (LP-005 Learning the noise covariance). In the graded free energy the two pieces are the fit and complexity summands.

Potential (row 4). In energy-based models and in the diffusion family, “energy” means an unnormalised negative log-density Eθ​(z) on the joint space, with pθ​∝e−Eθ​ (Energy-Based Learning). It is a different kind of object from row 1: the residual that vanishes on the relation is its gradient, rθ​(z)=∇z​Eθ​(z). Two consequences:

  • for the linear-Gaussian factor the two meet: E(x,y)=21​r⊤Q−1r (+ const), so the potential is the scalar pointwise energy of row 2;
  • for an energy network they do not: applying SquaredNorm to the residual ∇z​Eθ​ gives 21​∥∇z​Eθ​∥2, a squared gradient norm (the Gauss–Newton merit of the stationarity condition), not Eθ​.

Not these. Physical energies in the applications (electrical power, a molecular potential, mechanical work) are quantities in the model, not any of the rows above, even where a potential Eθ​ is fitted to them.

3. Energies on beliefs

Free energy of a factor (row 5). AutoBayes Definition 20, in vector form: the expected pointwise energy under the inversion minus the game entropy,

Fc(π,y)=E(x,a)∼cπ′​(y)​[lc(x,a,y)]−Hc(π,y).

For the Gaussian factors it is graded (fit, complexity, negentropy): fit is E[21​r⊤Q−1r], complexity the normaliser, negentropy =−H. In a graph the same quantity is computed from the incoming messages by local_free_energy; the game-level free_energy(f, π, y, …) passes the prior π as the message on the unobserved channel and y on the observed one (L-SPEC-001 The Factor Interface §4).

Scalar free energy (row 6). Row 5 scalarised; with the identity on each summand it is fit + complexity − entropy. The bridge to rows 2–3: at a point, fit equals the scalar pointwise energy; with a covariance it is that energy at the mean plus 21​tr(Q−1Covr) (tested in test/runtests.jl).

Bethe free energy (row 7). The free energy of a whole graph: factor free energies minus the overcounted variable entropies,

FBethe​=a∑​Fa​+v∑​(1−dv​)(−H(bv​)),

with dv​ the degree of variable v. Exact on a tree, where it equals the variational free energy, and with the prior factors in place (AutoBayes Remark 24) it equals −logp(data) when the beliefs are exact: the project’s main correctness test. On a loopy graph it is an approximation whose stationary points are the fixed points of loopy belief propagation (Yedidia, Freeman & Weiss). Its negative is the ELBO when the beliefs are a variational family.

4. Entropies

  • Game entropy (A) versus factor-belief entropy (B). A is a slot in a statistical game, the entropy of the inversion given a prior; B is the entropy of the factor’s joint belief in a graph. They coincide for a factor whose inversion is the factor belief; in a graph, B is what the Bethe formula uses.
  • Variable entropies (C) enter the Bethe free energy with counting number 1−dv​, which is negative for dv​≥2: they correct the overcounting of B, they are not regularisers.
  • Discrete versus continuous (D). Shannon entropy is ≥0 and intrinsic. Differential entropy can be negative, changes under a change of variables (by the log-Jacobian), and is relative to Lebesgue measure. Free energies of continuous beliefs are therefore comparable only between models over the same coordinates (LP-006 Gaussian entropies are relative to Lebesgue measure).
  • Categorical entropy (E). Perrone’s entropy is the divergence of a morphism from being deterministic. With the KL divergence it is Shannon entropy for discrete measures and infinite for every continuous one (Corollary 4.7): the intrinsic reason D needs a reference measure.
  • A Dirac has no entropy (variable_entropy(::DiracBelief) = 0), which is why factors whose inversion is a point (the diffusion and equilibrium factors) contribute energy only. A particle belief (SampleBelief) is an empirical measure with no density, so it has no differential entropy, and variable_entropy throws for it rather than guess.

Look-alikes that are not entropies.

  • Negentropy is −H: the negentropy summand is minus an entropy, so it is ≤0 for discrete beliefs and of either sign for continuous ones.
  • Cross-entropy Eq​[−logp] is an energy (the energy of a prior game), not an entropy; the score-matching term of the diffusion factor is filed this way (The Implicit Diffusion Factor as a Statistical Game §2).
  • Conditional entropy H(y∣x) appears when a likelihood factor’s output is unobserved: it cancels the expected energy, leaving −H(x) (local_free_energy(::LikelihoodFactor, …), predictive case).
  • Mutual information measures the laxness of parallel composition (AutoBayes Remark 26): it is a correction between free energies, not the entropy of anything.

5. Temperature

All of the above are at temperature 1. With p∝e−E/T the free energy is F=Eq​[E]−TH(q). As T→0 the entropy term vanishes, the optimal belief collapses to a point, and free-energy minimisation becomes energy minimisation (MAP; sum-product message passing becomes max-product). Dirac beliefs are this limit; a factor that returns them behaves as if at T=0 even inside a graph at T=1.

6. Checklist of confusions

  1. Energy (rows 1–4) versus free energy (rows 5–7): a function of points versus a functional of beliefs.
  2. Vector versus scalar: energy and free_energy return vectors; scalar_energy and scalar_free_energy return numbers. Scalarising twice was a real bug (½‖r‖² squared again in the DEQ and neural-ODE factors, fixed 2026-10-24).
  3. Residual versus potential: for an energy-based model the residual is ∇Eθ​, and its squared norm is not Eθ​.
  4. fit versus negative log-likelihood: they differ by complexity.
  5. Entropy versus negentropy: opposite signs.
  6. Bethe counting numbers: (1−dv​) multiplies −Hv​. The bethe_free_energy docstring had the sign wrong until 2026-10-24; the code was right.
  7. Differential entropies are coordinate-dependent and can be negative.
  8. The training loss need not be any free energy above: score matching, a bilevel prediction loss or a Lagrangian are separate objectives (Training Energy-Based Models).

7. Derivative orders

Whether a model is written as a residual rθ​ or as a potential Eθ​ shifts every derivative order by one, which matters for cost:

operationresidual rθ​(z)=0potential Eθ​(z), r=∇z​Eθ​
evaluate the defectrθ​∇Eθ​ (1st)
Gaussian (Laplace) message precision∂z​rθ​ (1st)∇zz2​Eθ​ (2nd)
backward pass by the implicit function theoremsolve with (∂z​rθ​)⊤ (1st)solve with ∇zz2​Eθ​ (2nd)
differentiate the Laplace precision or entropy2nd3rd

For a potential the Hessian is symmetric, so the matrix of the backward solve and the precision of the Laplace message are the same matrix (with the anchor precisions added to both). Caching it at the end of inference gives the Gaussian message and the backward pass for one Hessian. Differentiating through 21​logdet∇2Eθ​ needs third derivatives; training by score matching or by a point-prediction loss through the implicit function theorem avoids it.
