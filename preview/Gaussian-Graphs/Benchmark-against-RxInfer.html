Benchmark against RxInfer

12 Nov 20267 min read

  • benchmark

benchmark comparison

Lenticulum against RxInfer, and both against exact references, on two models: a Kalman smoother and a mixture of two linear regressions. On the smoother both are exact to 10−10 and agree with each other to about 10−10. Lenticulum is within a factor 1.7 of RxInfer up to T=100 and 4.3–5.8 times slower at T=1000–2000, because its cost grows faster than linearly in the size of the graph. On the mixture, Lenticulum’s fit_em is exactly maximum-likelihood EM; RxInfer’s variational estimates are slightly worse in likelihood but 9 to 10 times faster. Hand-written closed-form EM is about 2 000 and 4 900 times faster than Lenticulum and about 220 and 490 times faster than RxInfer.

Sources: Bagaev, Podusenko & de Vries, RxInfer: A Julia package for reactive real-time Bayesian inference, JOSS 2023 (the library compared, version 6.1.3). code: bench/rxinfer_baseline.jl.

Bibliography: Bagaev et al. 2023

Theory (CT-ML wiki): Variational Free Energy

Setup

Run julia -t auto --project=bench bench/rxinfer_baseline.jl (BENCH_PART=ssm or mix for one part). Measured 2026-11-11 on one machine (8 threads, -t auto; Julia 1.13.1, RxInfer 6.1.3), both parts in one run; times are warm (after one run), best of five. The script checks, and stops otherwise, that infer! converged and that fit_em’s free energy at its estimate is the score below.

1. A linear-Gaussian state-space model

A constant-velocity state in R2, position-only measurements, T steps (Xoshiro(T) data). Both libraries run belief propagation on a tree, which is exact, so their free energies must equal −logp(y) and their posteriors the conditional marginals. The references are a Kalman filter’s prediction-error decomposition in 256-bit arithmetic for −logp(y), and the dense joint solve in information form for the marginals.

What it shows.

  1. Both are exact, to 10−10 in −logp(y) and 10−11 in the means, and agree with each other to 1.2⋅10−10.
  2. Lenticulum’s cost grows faster than linearly. From T=1000 to 2000 the graph build takes 3.7 times as long, the free energy 3.6 times and infer! 3.0 times; RxInfer takes 2.6 (with F) and 2.5 times. The quadratic part is the one the Feature Backlog records for parameter keys looked up by name (LP-024: the free energy of 601, 1 201 and 2 401 factors in 7.7, 25.8 and 102 ms). Up to T=100 the two are within a factor 1.7.
  3. RxInfer needs an option beyond T=1000. At T=2000 its default run overflowed the stack; its documented remedy, limit_stack_depth = 100, is used there.

Getting the reference right. Two dense formulas for −logp(y) were wrong at T=1000 while the libraries agreed with each other to 2⋅10−11. The covariance form was off by 2.8⋅10−7: the prior covariance of a constant-velocity state grows like T3. The information form was off by 1.2⋅10−5: it is a difference of terms of order 103–104. The Kalman filter in BigFloat is exact.

2. A mixture of two linear regressions

300 or 3 000 points, x∼U(−3,3), line k with weights (0.5,0.5), y=ak​x+ck​+σk​ε, a=(1,−1), c=(0,0.5), σ=(0.2,0.5) (Xoshiro(11), the data of lib/Lenticulum.jl/examples/regime_lines.md). One start for all: a=(0.5,−0.5), c=0, σ=1, w=(0.5,0.5).

  • EM (hand): closed-form EM for a mixture of regressions, the reference.
  • Lenticulum: one RegimeFactor per point with tied parameters, fit_em (the closed-form M-steps of src/m_steps.jl), as in test/em.jl. Graph building not timed.
  • RxInfer: priors θk​∼N(0,100I), τk​∼Γ(1,1), w∼Dir(1,1), the means dot(X[i], θk), a NormalMixture likelihood, mean-field VMP over all variables. Point estimates are posterior means, σk​=1/Eτk​​. Run until its free energy changes by less than 10−8 per iteration.

The estimators differ: EM gives the maximum-likelihood parameters, VMP a variational posterior under priors. The common score is −logp(y∣x,θ^) at each estimate, labels matched.

The first table of 2026-11-11 had 1 196.3 and 31 635.8 ms for the unplated graph and 132.6 and 3 197.4 ms for RxInfer; what made the unplated graph faster since was not measured step by step. Lenticulum’s time excludes building its graph, 2.6 and 28.7 ms; RxInfer’s includes creating its model. RxInfer’s own free energy at convergence is 305.24 and 2 666.56: a bound under its priors, not comparable with the column above.

What it shows.

  1. Lenticulum’s EM is EM: the same iterates and the same maximum to the printed digits, as test/em.jl asserts step for step.
  2. It is slow. Unplated, 0.80 s against 0.6 ms for the closed form at N=300, 10.1 s against 5.8 ms at N=3000: 6.3 and 3.2 times RxInfer, and ten times the data costs 12.6 times the time. As a plate (I6 of the Feature Backlog) it is 2.2 and 2.6 times faster, 0.36 s and 3.8 s, 2.8 and 1.2 times RxInfer, and ten times the data costs 10.7 times the time. Even batched, each replica’s message is built as its own belief (no block arithmetic for these factors); where the rest of the time goes was not profiled.
  3. RxInfer’s estimates are close but not maximum likelihood. At N=300 its σ^1​ is 0.228 against 0.193, which costs 3.5 nats in −logp(y∣x,θ^). At N=3000 it is 0.48 nats. Partly that is the estimator: 1/Eτ​ is not the maximiser, and the posterior is mean-field. Which part is which was not separated.

Caveats

  • One machine, one data set per size, one start. Both EMs kept the start’s label order; RxInfer’s came out swapped and were matched by the slopes.
  • RxInfer was run as its documentation describes, not tuned: no custom schedules, limit_stack_depth only where the default failed. Lenticulum’s graph-building time is excluded in part 2 (reported beside it) and included in part 1’s total.
  • Not measured: compilation time (first run), memory, streaming use, graphs with loops.
