cluster

04 Nov 202620 min read

implementation

Cluster nodes: cluster(g, name, factors) replaces a set of factor nodes by one node that runs exact inference inside (enumeration of the discrete states, Gaussian elimination of the continuous ones) and looks like one factor outside, with exact messages and a closed-form local free energy. A whole switching chain in one cluster has the free energy −logp(y) to 10−12; three switches in one cluster send the messages of E7’s hand-built block factor.

Sources: Yedidia, Freeman & Weiss, Constructing Free-Energy Approximations and Generalized Belief Propagation Algorithms, IEEE Trans. Inf. Theory 51(7), 2005 (regions; here a region is solved exactly); de Jong, The Diffuse Kalman Filter, Ann. Statist. 19(2), 1991 (the diffuse likelihood: flat directions). code: cluster.jl; Lenticulum src/switching.jl (cluster_form for the constraint factors and GaussianPrior); tests: ../test/cluster.jl, Lenticulum test/cluster.jl

Bibliography: Yedidia et al. 2005 · de Jong 1991

Theory (CT-ML wiki): Variational Free Energy

Implements: L-SPEC-009 Cluster Nodes, Extending Mycelium §5.

What a cluster computes

Every factor in a cluster states itself through cluster_form(factor, ps) as a conditionally linear-Gaussian term: a residual r=∑c​Ac​xc​−C:,k​ with noise N(0,Q) and weight wk​, the column k read from one discrete channel or fixed, so that −logf=21​r⊤Q−1r+21​logdet2πQ−logwk​. A discrete-only factor (a categorical prior) has no rows. Mycelium implements the form for PriorFactor (categorical or proper Gaussian belief), Lenticulum for LinearConstraintFactor, SwitchingConstraintFactor and GaussianPrior.

For a joint assignment a of the cluster’s discrete variables, the product of the factors and the incoming messages is exp(ha⊤​x−21​x⊤Jx+ca​) over the free continuous variables, with J=∑f​Gf⊤​Qf−1​Gf​+∑v​Λv​ the same for every assignment (the forms share A and Q across outcomes), ha​=∑f​Gf⊤​Qf−1​cf,a​+∑v​ηv​, and ca​ collecting the quadratic constants, the normalisers, the log-weights and the incoming discrete log-probabilities. Points (data, pins) are substituted into the constants; a Gaussian-mixture input is collapsed to its moments.

  • Message to a continuous channel t. Eliminating the other continuous variables (a Cholesky of Jrr​, shared) leaves per assignment exp(η~​a⊤​xt​−21​xt⊤​Λ~xt​) with Λ~=Jtt​−Jtr​Jrr−1​Jrt​ and the mass ca​+21​hr⊤​Jrr−1​hr​−21​logdetJrr​+2nr​​log2π. The message is the mixture over the assignments, normalised components when Λ~ is positive definite, unnormalised exponentials otherwise, as the switching factor’s.
  • Message to a discrete channel. P(k)∝∑a:ad​=k​exp(ca​+log∫exp(ha⊤​x−21​x⊤Jx)dx).
  • Local free energy. The belief b(x,a) is, given a, the Gaussian N(J−1ha​,J−1), and qa​∝elogZa​. So H(b)=H(q)+H(x∣a) is closed form, and Eb​[−log∏f​f] sums each factor’s expected quadratic and its normaliser. Graded as (fit, complexity, negentropy), as the switching factor’s local free energy, so a cluster of switches and its block factor agree term by term.
  • Gradient. factor_belief freezes q, the means per assignment and the covariance; belief_free_energy re-evaluates every factor’s form at ps (LP-020). At an exact belief this is the gradient of −logp. The bookkeeping of which outcome and which channels each factor reads is _cluster_layout, non-differentiable.

cluster(g, name, factors) builds the new graph: the variables touched only by the clustered factors become internal, the others are the node’s channels (named after the variables, on bidirectional edges); a variable is discrete when a clustered factor switches on it (cluster_switch). The node’s parameters are a NamedTuple keyed by the clustered factors’ parameter keys, so tied factors inside keep one entry (cluster_parameters rearranges a parameter tree). cluster_marginal reads the marginal of any cluster variable, internal ones included.

Tests

Lenticulum test/cluster.jl:

  • the switching chain of test/mixture_rules.jl (T=5) with every factor but the data in one cluster: the free energy equals −logp(y) by enumeration to 10−12 (relative), every xt​‘s marginal mean and variance match the enumerated posterior to 10−12, and the gradient in the tied offsets matches central differences of −logp(y) (reference differences 10−6, ForwardDiff and Zygote 10−7);
  • E7’s additive wiring (z=u1​+u2​+u3​+ε, one two-outcome switch per ul​): the cluster of the three switches sends the message of the hand-built 8-outcome block factor (log-densities to 10−12), the same loopy schedule gives the same marginals on both graphs to 10−10, and the two nodes’ local free energies agree term by term to 10−10; a cluster of one switch is that switch;
  • the same wiring with the sum and the switches in one cluster: the plain graph has no free energy (mixture marginals at the ul​), the clustered one has −logp(z) to 10−12;
  • refusals: a factor without a form, a missing name, a taken name, a key tied outside, a plate.

Mycelium test/cluster.jl: priors only, the message is the product of the Gaussian priors, an internal categorical keeps its prior, and the refusals.

Grouping by overlap and several clusters at once (2026-11-03)

cluster(g, [name₁ => factors₁, name₂ => factors₂, …]; params) makes several cluster nodes in one call. With params they read one parameter entry, tied: one cluster per data point of one model (examples/structure_edits.jl). Without it a parameter key shared between groups is refused, since each node would hold its own copy. cluster_parameters(gc, ps) converts for every node.

overlap_groups(C, maxsize) is E7’s greedy grouping (Composing Stochastic Factors): start from singletons, merge the two groups whose largest pairwise overlap is largest while the merged group fits and the overlap is positive. Lenticulum’s switch_overlaps(g, ps, sum, switches) gives the overlaps of switching factors feeding one linear factor: each switch’s offset directions mapped through the sum’s coefficients, ∣cos∣ in the metric of the sum’s total noise R=Qsum​+∑l​Al​Ql​Al⊤​. For binary switches this is E7’s ∣Glm​∣/Gll​Gmm​​, G=W⊤R−1W: test/cluster.jl compares both to 10−12 and the groups with E7’s own function, copied as the oracle, for group sizes 2, 3 and 4; two planted overlapping pairs end up in the same groups.

Whether it helps, bench/cluster_overlap.jl (d=8, eight binary switches in four overlapping pairs, interleaved so that index order splits them, 100 points from the model, 50 loopy sweeps), the mean over points of the mean ∣ΔP(kl​=2)∣ against the exact posterior (every factor in one cluster):

E-stepmean ∣ΔP∣max
one switching factor per switch0.09220.2376
clusters of the overlap groups, the switches only0.09220.2376
E7’s blocks of 4 in index order (partial sum + switches per cluster)0.09170.2433

Grouping by overlap cuts the error to a third, as in E7, but only with E7’s wiring: each block’s switches feed a partial sum sb​=∑l∈b​ul​ inside the block’s cluster, and the blocks’ sums feed the sum node. Clustering the switches alone changes nothing, to four digits: they meet only at the sum node, which collapses each ul​‘s mixture to its moments separately, so the cluster’s joint over its switches never reaches the coupling. Blocks that ignore overlap help as little.

Discrete tables, joint outcomes and impossible assignments (2026-11-03)

A ClusterForm’s switch may be a tuple of discrete channels; the outcome is then their joint state, in column-major order (the first channel fastest, LinearIndices). TableFactor(dims, logtable) uses this: a fixed factor f(k1​,…,kn​)=exp(logtable[k1​,…,kn​]) over discrete variables, a discrete-only term (m=0) inside a cluster. Outside one it sends exact belief-propagation messages (the table summed against the other channels’ categorical messages, or sliced at an observed state); it has no local free energy there.

Its entries may be −∞, so a table can tie discrete variables together. That is how examples/structure_clusters.jl writes E3b’s switch over sums: each switch reads an outcome ql​=(bit,j) with 2J states, and a table over (j,ql​) allows only the ql​ of cluster j. Enumerating every joint state would cost J(2J)L assignments where J2L are possible, so _cluster_joint now adds every factor’s log-weights first and drops the assignments of weight zero before the Gaussian part (the precisions and solves). The beliefs keep the surviving assignments only. Since 2026-11-04 the enumeration itself is pruned (_enumerate_assignments): the assignments are built one discrete variable at a time, a factor’s log-weights are added once all its switch channels are assigned, and a partial assignment of weight zero is dropped there. The 2^16 cap now counts the possible assignments, not the full product: 18 binary variables tied equal by tables (2^18 joint states, 2 possible) form one cluster (test/cluster.jl, “pruned enumeration”; before, the cap refused it). It does not speed up the plated example: at (4,3), 2 048 joint states of which 32 are possible, a fitting step took 0.155 s against 0.158 s before (a scratch timing), since the batched path builds one joint per plate group.

A cluster’s message merges equal components: assignments the target does not see (an internal discrete variable that does not move it) gave repeated components with split weights; they are now one component with the summed weight, and a message whose components are all equal is one Gaussian (test/cluster.jl, Mycelium’s side). In belief_free_energy a −∞ log-weight met a weight of exactly 0 and gave 0 · (-Inf) = NaN; such entries are set to 0 before the product (_finite_logw), which is the 0log0=0 convention, and no AD backend sees the infinity.

Tests (test/cluster.jl): three categorical variables with two tables (one −∞ entry each) in one cluster give F=−logZ and the marginals of enumeration to 10−12, and belief propagation through the tables (the graph is a tree) the same marginals; the observed-state message and the column order. In Lenticulum, the switch over sums with J=2, L=2 in one cluster gives F=−logp(z) to 10−14 (a scratch check), and the example checks it at (3,2) on 200 points (9⋅10−13) along with the gradient against central differences (examples/structure_clusters.md).

Cluster nodes over a plate (2026-11-04)

cluster(g, plate, name, factors; params) clusters the template factors factors of every replica of plate into one tied node per replica, and the graph keeps the plate. Inside each node the variables carry their template names (:z, not z[3]), so every replica has the same channels, which a plate group needs. as_plate(g, name, n) (plates.jl) records the nodes named x[i] of an unplated graph as a plate; structure edits refuse plates, so an edited graph gets its plate back this way.

Tied clusters differ only in what their replica’s messages bring. When every replica has the same factors (compared field by field) and its inputs the same shape (which channels are points, the input precisions, the discrete inputs), replica i‘s joint is replica 1’s with Hi​=H1​+(hi​−h1​)1⊤ and ci​=c1​+(δi​−δ1​): h collects the Gaussian inputs’ η and the points’ shifts −Gf⊤​Qf−1​sf​, δ the terms sf⊤​Qf−1​Cf,a​−21​sf⊤​Qf−1​sf​. So factor_messages builds one joint, its precision’s factorisations and the surviving assignments once (cluster_batch.jl), and factor_beliefs returns FrozenClusterBeliefs, whose belief_free_energies evaluates all replicas as a few stacked matrix products: what the parameter gradient differentiates. Replicas that differ fall back to the per-node methods.

Tests (test/cluster.jl, “Cluster nodes over a plate”): the switch over sums (J=2, a table) on 12 points, plated against the same graph without the plate record: free energy equal to 10−10 and to −∑i​logp(zi​) by enumeration, the batched beliefs taken, the gradient equal to 10−8 with ForwardDiff and Zygote, batched messages equal to one by one, the fallback chosen when one replica’s input is a Gaussian instead of a point; as_plate’s refusals. On 50 points at (3,2) a scratch timing gave sweeps plus free energy 3.7 times and the gradient 6 times faster than the unplated clusters, at equal values (9⋅10−13, 10−11). The examples’ timings are in examples/structure_edits.md and examples/structure_clusters.md.

Per-outcome coefficients and noise (2026-11-04)

A ClusterForm may give an entry of A as an m×d×K array and Q as an m×m×K array: the factor’s coefficients Ac,k​ and noise Qk​ then depend on its outcome k, as in Lenticulum’s RegimeFactor (src/regime.md). Given the assignment a of the cluster’s discrete variables the continuous ones are still jointly Gaussian, but the precision

Ja​=J0​+f varying∑​Gf,kf​(a)⊤​Qf,kf​(a)−1​Gf,kf​(a)​

now depends on a through the outcomes kf​(a) of the varying factors only (J0​ holds the incoming messages and the factors whose A and Q do not vary). The assignments are grouped into precision classes, one per combination of those outcomes; each class is factorised once (_precision_classes, the factorisation cache is keyed by class). Without a varying factor there is one class and every computation is the one above.

  • Messages. To a discrete channel nothing changes but the class-wise log-integrals. To a continuous channel each class gives its own precision Λκ​=Jtt​−Jtr​Jrr−1​Jrt​, so the message is a mixture whose components need not share a precision. Components are merged when both their precision and their natural mean are equal. A proper component is normalised and its mass moved into the weight; an improper one is the unnormalised exponential exp(η⊤x−x⊤Λx/2) its weight multiplies, which is how Mycelium’s mixture products read it (gaussian.jl, _improper_overlap). So improper components with different precisions are weighted consistently. (The first version refused them, after switching.md §5; that concern is about a factor that projects flat inputs out, not about an exact message.)
  • Belief and free energy. FrozenClusterBelief holds one covariance Σκ​ and one entropy H(x∣κ) per class and the class of each assignment. In the free energy a factor whose A and Q vary is evaluated per outcome group: its residual means with Ac,k​, its trace term ∑κ​qk,κ​tr(Qk−1​Gk​Σκ​Gk⊤​) with qk,κ​ the belief’s mass on the assignments of outcome k in class κ, and its 21​logdet2πQk​ weighted by the mass on outcome k. The negentropy is −H(q)−∑a​qa​H(x∣κ(a)).
  • Plates. Replicas with a varying factor are not batched (_cluster_batchable is false): the data terms Gk⊤​Qk−1​s would differ per class, and the batched path assumes one precision. They take the per-replica path, with the same results.
  • One factor outside a cluster. as_cluster(f) is a factor as a one-factor cluster over its own channels. A factor stated only by a cluster_form gets its messages and Bethe free energy from it (Lenticulum’s RegimeFactor does).

Tests: Lenticulum test/regime.jl. A mixture of factor analysers (x∼N(0,I), z=Wk​x+μk​+εk​, three regimes with different Wk​ and Qk​) in one cluster gives F=−logp(z) to 10−12, the regime posterior and the three posteriors of x (each with its own covariance) to 10−12; the gradient in A, b, logw and the noise’s Cholesky factors agrees with central differences of −logp(z) to 10−6 (ForwardDiff and Zygote); over a plate of four points the per-replica path is taken and is exact.

Flat directions (2026-11-04)

A direction of the continuous variables that no factor in the cluster and no incoming message determines is flat: the precision (Jκ​, or Jrr​ when the target’s message eliminates the other variables) is singular there. Until 2026-11-04 the node refused it. It now integrates such directions out in the diffuse limit: a vague prior N(0,s2I), s→∞, with the factor (2πs2)k/2 of the k flat directions dropped. On the range of J (an orthonormal basis U, eigenvalues λ),

log∫exp(h⊤x−21​x⊤Jx)dx→21​h⊤J+h−21​logpdetJ+2n−k​log2π,

which needs h⊥kerJ: every quadratic term vanishes on kerJ (J is a sum of positive semidefinite terms), so only a linear term of an incoming message can reach a flat direction, and that integral diverges; it is refused. The dropped factor is the same for all assignments only if they leave equally many directions flat; otherwise the node refuses (in the vague limit the assignments with more flat directions would take all the weight, which is rarely what a model means). This is the diffuse treatment of state-space models (de Jong 1991, the diffuse likelihood) and what the constraint factors do with a flat input (constraint.md §4.1).

  • Where it matters. A boundary channel with no incoming message yet (the first sweep of a loopy schedule), a factor outside a cluster with a flat input (as_cluster, Lenticulum’s RegimeFactor), and combinations of variables the model never determines (only u−w enters, so u+w is flat).
  • Belief and free energy. The frozen belief holds M=J+H and Σ=J+ per class, the flat directions at mean 0 and dropped; H(x∣κ) is the entropy on the range. Every factor’s G annihilates kerJ, so the fit terms are the limits of the vague ones, and their gradient in θ at the frozen belief is the vague gradient’s limit (a term GNN⊤/ε with GN=0 vanishes).
  • Marginals. cluster_marginal of a variable in a flat class is the information form of the other variables eliminated; a variable with a flat direction gets an improper component (zero precision there) rather than a covariance with zeros.
  • Deciding flatness. An eigenvalue at most 10−10 times the largest is flat. Cholesky can succeed on a singular J (rounding leaves a pivot of 10−15, and the first test then gave variances of 6⋅1014 and a free energy 18.7 off), so its smallest pivot is checked against the same threshold. A precision that weak but meant (a prior of standard deviation 105 beside factors of precision 1) is taken as flat, which shifts the free energy by the dropped constant.

Tests: Lenticulum test/cluster_flat.jl. With only u−w determined, the free energy equals the vague graph’s (s=104) less 21​log2πs2 to 10−6 and the closed form −logp(z)+21​log2 to 10−12; the switch posterior and the gradient (ForwardDiff, Zygote) equal the vague ones, and u‘s marginal is improper of zero precision. A RegimeFactor with x∈R3 seen through 2×3 maps sends the message to the regime of the vague input to 10−7 and the closed form wk​/detWk​Wk⊤​​ to 10−12; a linear term along a flat direction and regimes with different numbers of flat directions are refused; improper components of rank one with different precisions have the closed-form Λk​, ηk​ and weights.

Frozen parameter paths (2026-11-04)

Until 2026-11-04 cluster refused clustered nodes with frozen parameter paths, so the structure examples held the sum node’s coefficients fixed by hand in their fitting loops. A clustered node’s freeze now carries over to the cluster node, under its parameter key: (key, path...) for each path, (key,) for a node frozen whole (or a factor that is isfrozen). The cluster node’s parameters are keyed by those keys, so the paths name the same coordinates, and the usual pinning (_factor_parameters) zeroes their gradient on every path, the batched plate path included. Tied groups must freeze the same paths. Test: Lenticulum test/cluster.jl, “Cluster nodes keep their factors’ frozen parameter paths” (zero gradient in the frozen A, the other gradients unchanged, ForwardDiff and Zygote).

What it does not do

  • Factors outside the conditionally linear-Gaussian class. A factor needs cluster_form (its A and Q may depend on its outcome since 2026-11-04, above). DataFactor stays outside: data reaches the cluster as point messages.
  • Flat directions with unequal counts, or reached by a linear term. Refused (above). Flat directions in equal number are integrated out in the diffuse limit.
  • Enumeration is exponential. At most 216 joint discrete states of nonzero weight (pruned as they are built, above); equal components of a message are merged, but nothing reduces the number of distinct ones. Precision classes add one factorisation each.
  • Plates, other rules, other couplings. cluster(g, groups) refuses graphs with plates (cluster(g, plate, name, factors) clusters a plate’s factors, on a graph with one plate), nodes that are not on belief propagation, a parameter key tied to a node outside, and edges that are not bidirectional, diagonal and attached. The cluster node itself is a belief-propagation node; differentiating through inference (adjoint = :implicit) is not tested with it.
  • Grouping rewires nothing. overlap_groups chooses the groups; putting a partial sum per group into the graph, which is what makes the groups pay (below), is an edit of the graph. For switches feeding a Lenticulum linear factor, Lenticulum’s partial_sums makes it (src/switching.md §14); Mycelium itself knows no sum factor.

Difficulties

  1. Tied parameters. The first version keyed the node’s parameters by factor name and refused tied factors, which refused the switching chain at once (its observation and dynamics factors are tied). The node now keys them by parameter key, and refuses only a key shared with a node outside the cluster.
  2. A comparison the test could not make. A cluster of the three switches and the block factor were first compared by the graph free energy; neither graph has one, since the ul​ have mixture marginals and Mycelium no mixture entropy. The test compares the nodes’ local free energies instead, and a separate test shows that clustering the ul​ in removes the gap.
  3. Review (Gemini via agy, 2026-11-03). Three findings were fixed: a factor channel left unconnected inside a cluster gave a KeyError (now refused by cluster, tested); a discrete input with no possible state gave an empty-collection error instead of an ArgumentError; the batched mixture product did not check the replica count. Two were not bugs: _indefinite depends on the precision only, so a shared one is one test; and Zygote through belief_free_energy works (the chain’s gradient is tested with it, and _cluster_layout is non-differentiable in the ChainRulesCore extension, which the reviewed diff did not show).
  4. Review of the grouping (Gemini via agy, 2026-11-03). Fixed: a cluster’s parameter key could equal the key of a node left outside, and cluster_parameters would then overwrite that node’s entry (refused now, tested); tied nodes compared their parameter keys in order, so the same factors listed in another order were refused (compared as sets now); switch_overlaps took the directions of a switch with three or more outcomes from the first outcome only (every pair now). Documented: the metric counts the noise of the sum and of the listed switches only.

Review of 2026-11-05 (Mistral via vibe)

A read-only review of the EM code found that batched cluster nodes treated an observed discrete boundary variable as “a point” without its state (_input_shape). Replicas that observed different states were batched on replica 1’s surviving assignments, so their messages, free energies and EM statistics were those of replica 1’s state. Reproduced in Lenticulum test/cluster.jl (“Batched cluster nodes with an observed switch that differs between replicas”): a batched free energy of 54.76 against the exact 71.59, and the statistics’ masses [6, 0] against [3, 3]. The observed state is now part of the shape, so such replicas go replica by replica. The defect dates from the batched path (2026-11-04); the examples observe no discrete variable, so their results are unaffected.
