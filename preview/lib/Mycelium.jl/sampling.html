sampling

10 Nov 20265 min read

implementation

Passing points instead of beliefs. A Rao-Blackwellised Gibbs sampler draws chosen variables (typically discrete switches) from their exact conditionals, integrating the rest out by belief propagation with the drawn ones pinned. A sample buffer keeps the samples, weighs them by a filter, forgets old ones, and averages energies, residual moments and Monte Carlo EM gradients without losing the structure of a multivariate energy.

Sources: Wei & Tanner, A Monte Carlo Implementation of the EM Algorithm and the Poor Man’s Data Augmentation Algorithms, JASA 85(411), 1990 (Monte Carlo EM); Delyon, Lavielle & Moulines, Convergence of a Stochastic Approximation Version of the EM Algorithm, Ann. Statist. 27(1), 1999 (the decaying filter). code: sampling.jl, messages.jl (pins), free_energy.jl; tests: test/sampling.jl, Lenticulum’s test/switching_constraint.jl and test/lp020.jl

Bibliography: Wei & Tanner 1990 · Delyon et al. 1999

Theory (CT-ML wiki): Variational Free Energy · Bayesian Inversion

Implements §6b of Extending Mycelium.

Pins

pin!(store, g, :v, value) makes variable v observed for the time being: its marginal, and every message it sends to a factor, is DiracBelief(value), whatever the factors send it. Pins live in the MessageStore, so a sampler moves them between updates without rebuilding the graph. reset! clears messages and keeps pins. Only marginal and excluded_marginal read them, and every reader of beliefs goes through those two (variable → factor messages, the Bethe variable terms, the read-out). The free-energy functions see a pinned variable as clamped data.

The sampler

GibbsSampler(g, sampled) names the variables to draw. One update of variable v:

  1. pin every other sampled variable at its current point and release v;
  2. clear the messages and run the schedule (a tree schedule by default);
  3. draw v from its marginal (sample_belief: Dirac, categorical, proper Gaussian, mixture).

The marginal of v is its exact conditional given the pins whenever the graph without the pinned variables is a tree with closed-form messages. In a conditional-Gaussian graph with its discrete variables sampled, that is the case, and the continuous variables are integrated out exactly, never sampled (Rao-Blackwellisation). Blocks are sampled jointly by making them one variable, e.g. one switching factor with 2B outcomes for B overlapping switches. That is the mixing remedy for switches that explain each other away, the same block structure that fixes the pair-moment bias of factorised beliefs.

gibbs_init draws the first points in order, each from its marginal with the earlier ones pinned. For the later ones that marginal is only approximate; it starts the chain and nothing more. Each update reruns the whole schedule, which is simple and costs one inference per variable.

The buffer: kept, filtered, forgotten

A SampleBuffer(filter; horizon) stores, per sample:

  • the pins;
  • the graded local free energies of every factor and the variable corrections;
  • the total (the pinned graph’s scalar free energy);
  • every factor’s residual mean and covariance (factor_residual_moments, implemented by Lenticulum’s constraint factors);
  • an optional weight;
  • the parameter step at which it was drawn.

The filter weighs the samples by age:

  • WindowFilter(n): the last n, equal weights;
  • DecayFilter(λ): λage, which with λ moved towards 1 is stochastic-approximation EM.

advance! counts a parameter step and forgets samples older than horizon steps, since samples from old parameters bias the gradient.

The queries obey the averaging requirement of Extending Mycelium §6b:

  • energy_moments: the weighted mean and variance of every graded component, of each sample’s already scalarised local energies. Nothing is averaged before its scalarisation.
  • residual_moments(buffer, factor): the mean and the full covariance of the factor’s residual vector, by the law of total covariance (mean of within-sample covariances plus covariance of within-sample means). For a squared-norm scalarisation these are exact sufficient statistics, E∥r∥W2​=∥rˉ∥W2​+tr(WCovr).
  • free_energy_mean: the weighted mean of the totals, each −logp(data,pins) on an exact pinned graph.
  • (sample_gradient is in MyceliumLearning, sample_gradient, since 2026-11-10.)
  • sample_gradient: the weighted mean of each sample’s gradient of its total at the current parameters.

The gradient

sample_gradient pins each stored sample, reruns inference at the current parameters, and takes the gradient of the total (−logp(data,pins)) at those parameters. With exact posterior weights it equals the gradient of the exact −logp(data) (Lenticulum’s test, against finite differences of the enumerated likelihood). With posterior samples it is the Monte Carlo EM gradient.

By default (since 2026-11-01) each sample’s pinned inference is followed by free_energy_gradient, which holds the factor beliefs fixed (LP-020 The fixed-message parameter gradient is not the free-energy gradient). That is one inference per sample, and exact once the inference has converged, as on a pinned tree. The first version differentiated through the inference instead: one inference per sample and, with reference differences, per parameter coordinate too. It stays as through_inference = true, needs no factor hook, and is what the default is checked against.

Tested

  • Mycelium’s test/sampling.jl: pins (marginal, free energy, reset! keeps them, unpin!); sample_belief against its distributions; the sampler on a categorical prior; window and decay weights; per-component energy moments; forgetting by horizon; the errors.
  • Lenticulum’s test/switching_constraint.jl, on two overlapping switches into a sum node:
    • the sampler’s frequencies against the enumerated posterior;
    • with every configuration at its exact weight, each total is −logp(x,k), the residual moments match Gaussian conditioning, and the gradient matches finite differences of −logp(x) in c, b and logw. The default and through_inference = true agree to 10−6.

Not done

  • The cost of one full inference per update. A sampler should rerun only the part of the schedule that a pin change affects.
  • No convergence diagnostics (effective sample size, several chains) and no tempering.
  • Sampling of continuous variables works through sample_belief, but a pinned continuous variable makes its neighbours’ messages Dirac-driven, and nothing here checks that this mixes.
  • The buffer re-runs inference per stored sample for the gradient; caching the pinned stores would trade memory for time.
  • No parameter_step-style driver: the caller applies the gradient with an optimiser.
