Universal Approximation for Implicit Diffusion Learners

Universal Approximation for Implicit Diffusion Learners

24 Oct 202619 min read

  • theorem
  • derivation

theorem derivation

What an implicit diffusion learner can represent. For a fixed node set, the stable answers of an energy network’s learner approximate the stable answers of any target energy whose answers are nondegenerate, uniformly over a compact set of queries; an ε-network’s learner does the same for any target vector field. The answers move by at most 2δ/μ, where δ is the C1 distance of the residuals and μ measures how well-conditioned the target’s answers are. The theorem is about expressiveness. Whether training reaches such parameters is a separate question, and §6 lists what stays open.

The diffusion counterpart of Universal Approximation by Nash-Tognoli.

Sources: original to this vault (statement and proofs, not peer reviewed); uses Pinkus, Approximation Theory of the MLP Model in Neural Networks, Acta Numerica 1999 (Theorem 4.1, density in Cm); Hornik, Approximation Capabilities of Multilayer Feedforward Networks, Neural Networks 1991; for §6, Genovese, Perone-Pacifico, Verdinelli & Wasserman, Nonparametric ridge estimation, Annals of Statistics 2014

Bibliography: Pinkus 1999 · Hornik 1991 · Genovese et al. 2014

1. The objects

The learner of Implicit Diffusion Learners has fixed nodes (tk​,εk​,wk​), wk​>0, weight λ>0, and the field

gθ​(z)=k∑​wk​λk​(εθ​(αk​z+σk​εk​,tk​)−εk​),λk​=λσk​/αk​.

For an energy network, εθ​=σt​∇Eθ​, the field is gθ​=∇Uθ​ with

Uθ​(z)=k∑​ck​Eθ​(αk​z+σk​εk​,tk​)−ℓ⊤z,ck​=αk2​wk​λσk2​​>0,ℓ=k∑​wk​λk​εk​

(energy_network §3). A query clamps the coordinates C at zC​ and anchors the free coordinates F (with m=∣F∣) at z0,F​ with precisions ρF​∈[0,∞)m. Write p=(zC​,z0,F​) for its data and y=zF​ for the unknowns. Its residual is

rθ​(p,y)=gθ,F​(zC​,y)+ρF2​⊙(y−z0,F​),

and an answer is a zero y of rθ​(p,⋅). It is stable if sym∂y​rθ​(p,y)≻0, the flag stable of implicit_infer. For an energy network that is a strict local minimum of Uθ​(zC​,⋅)+21​∥ρF​⊙(y−z0,F​)∥2.

A target is any C1 vector field G:Rn→Rn, or for energy networks G=∇Ψ with Ψ∈C2. The query turns it into a residual rG​ in the same way.

Regularity. Let Q (query data) and Y⊂Rm (an answer window) be compact. A residual r, C1 on an open set N⊃Q×Y, is regular on (Q,Y) if

  • (R1) at every zero (p,y)∈Q×Y, J=∂y​r(p,y) is invertible and symJ has no zero eigenvalue;
  • (R2) r(p,y)=0 for p∈Q and y∈∂Y.

(R1) says every answer is nondegenerate and is clearly stable or clearly unstable. For a gradient, J is the Hessian block and (R1) says that every critical point in the fibre is nondegenerate (a Morse condition on the fibres). It excludes branch points (§5).

2. Answers persist

Lemma 1 (persistence of answers). Let r be regular on (Q,Y). There are a compact neighbourhood W⊂N of Q×Y and constants μ>0, δ0​>0 such that for every r~∈C1(N) with

Wsup​(∣r~−r∣+∥∂y​r~−∂y​r∥)≤δ≤δ0​

and every p∈Q, the zeros of r~(p,⋅) in Y and those of r(p,⋅) in Y are in a bijection that moves no zero by more than 2δ/μ and preserves stability. Moreover r~ is regular on (Q,Y) with constant μ/2.

Here μ=min over the zeros of r in Q×Y of min{σmin​(J),∣λmin​(symJ)∣}, and ∥⋅∥ denotes the operator 2-norm.

Proof. Let Z={(p,y)∈Q×Y:r(p,y)=0}; it is compact, and μ>0 because σmin​ and λmin​∘sym are continuous and positive in absolute value on Z by (R1). By (R2), d0​=min(p,y)∈Z​dist(y,Rm∖Y)>0. Choose r0​>0 so small that W={(p,y):p∈Q, dist(y,Y)≤r0​}⊂N and, by uniform continuity of ∂y​r on W, ∥∂y​r(p,y)−∂y​r(p,y0​)∥≤μ/4 whenever (p,y0​)∈Z and ∣y−y0​∣≤r0​.

Step 1: one zero per ball. Fix (p,y0​)∈Z, J0​=∂y​r(p,y0​), B=Bˉ(y0​,r0​), and let δ≤μ/4. For y,y′∈B, r~(p,y)−r~(p,y′)=M(y−y′) with M=∫01​∂y​r~(p,y′+s(y−y′))ds=J0​+E, ∥E∥≤μ/4+δ≤μ/2. So σmin​(M)≥μ/2>0 and r~(p,⋅) has at most one zero in B. (With r~=r: distinct zeros of r(p,⋅) are at least r0​ apart.)

Step 2: existence. Φ(y)=y−J0−1​r~(p,y) has ∥∂Φ∥=∥J0−1​(J0​−∂y​r~)∥≤μ−1⋅μ/2=1/2 on B, and ∣Φ(y0​)−y0​∣=∣J0−1​(r~−r)(p,y0​)∣≤δ/μ. With ρ=2δ/μ≤r0​, Φ maps Bˉ(y0​,ρ) into itself (ρ/2+δ/μ=ρ) and contracts, so it has a fixed point y~​: r~(p,y~​)=0 and ∣y~​−y0​∣≤2δ/μ.

Step 3: stability is kept. ∂y​r~(p,y~​)=J0​+E′ with ∥E′∥≤μ/2. By Weyl’s inequality ∣λmin​(sym(J0​+E′))−λmin​(symJ0​)∣≤μ/2<∣λmin​(symJ0​)∣: the sign is the same, and both σmin​ and ∣λmin​∣ are at least μ/2.

Step 4: no other zeros. The set S={(p,y)∈Q×Y:∣y−y0​∣≥r0​ for all (p,y0​)∈Z} is closed: its complement in Q×Y is open, because by the implicit function theorem (J0​ invertible, y0​ interior to Y) each zero continues to nearby p′. So S is compact, r=0 on it, and c=minS​∣r∣>0. For δ<c, every zero of r~(p,⋅) in Y lies within r0​ of a zero y0​ of r(p,⋅), and by Step 1 it is the y~​ of Step 2 for that y0​.

Take δ0​<min{μ/4, c, μr0​/4, μd0​/2}. Then ρ≤r0​/2, so different y0​ (at least r0​ apart) get different y~​; ρ<d0​, so y~​∈Y; and by Step 4 every zero of r~(p,⋅) in Y is reached. □

The constant is effective: an answer moves by at most 2δ/μ, where μ is the worst conditioning of the target’s answers. Near a branch point μ→0 (§5), and so does the guarantee.

3. The node sum can realise any target

The network enters Uθ​ only through the node sum. The question is whether every target energy is U for some E. It is, on a compact set, even though the nodes of one level see the network at shifted points (antithetic pairs see it at αz±σε).

Lemma 2 (inverting a finite sum of shifts). Let s1​,…,sq​∈Rn be distinct, γ1​,…,γq​=0, and G^∈Ck(Rn;Rd) with compact support. There is H∈Ck(Rn;Rd) with

j=1∑q​γj​H(z+sj​)=G^(z)for all z.

Proof. Pick v with the values ⟨sj​,v⟩ pairwise distinct (avoid finitely many hyperplanes) and number the shifts so that ⟨s1​,v⟩ is the largest. Let d=minj≥2​⟨s1​−sj​,v⟩>0 and m0​=minz∈suppG^​⟨z,v⟩+⟨s1​,v⟩. Define H=0 on {⟨u,v⟩<m0​} and

H(u)=γ1​1​(G^(u−s1​)−j≥2∑​γj​H(u−s1​+sj​))

elsewhere. The points u−s1​+sj​ lie at least d lower in the direction v, so by induction over the slabs {⟨u,v⟩<m0​+Nd}, H is well defined there and equals a finite linear combination of translates of G^. It is therefore Ck. On {⟨u,v⟩<m0​} the formula also gives 0, since G^(u−s1​)=0 there and the other terms lie lower still. So the formula holds everywhere, and at u=z+s1​ it is the claimed identity. □

Proposition 3 (exact realisation). Let K⊂Rn be compact and fix a level t∗ of the nodes. For every Ψ∈Ck(Rn) there is E∗∈Ck with UE∗​=Ψ on K, and E∗(⋅,t)=0 at every other node level. For every G∈Ck(Rn;Rn) there is a noise predictor ε∗ with gε∗​=G on K.

Proof (energy). Let I∗={k:tk​=t∗} and sk​=σ∗εk​/α∗. Group equal shifts, adding their ck​ into γj​>0. Take a smooth cutoff χ=1 on K with compact support, and apply Lemma 2 to G^=χ⋅(Ψ+ℓ⊤z). Set E∗(u,t∗)=H(u/α∗), so that E∗(α∗z+σ∗εk​,t∗)=H(z+sk​), and make E∗(⋅,t)=0 at the other levels with a smooth factor in t. Then UE∗​=G^−ℓ⊤z=Ψ on K. The field case is the same with d=n, weights wk​λk​ and G^=χ⋅(G+∑k​wk​λk​εk​). □

With noisefree_nodes (ε=0, one node) this is just E∗=(Ψ+ℓ⊤z)/c. With a single antithetic pair (q=2, s1,2​=±s) it is an alternating sum, H(u)=γ−1∑i≥0​(−1)iG^(u+(2i+1)s), whose Ck norm on a compact set grows at most linearly in the number of slabs diam(K)/∣s∣. For q≥3 equal-weight shifts (γj​=γ1​), the slab recurrence has amplification factor q−1≥2 per slab of width d∝σ∗, so ∥H∥Ck​ on K is finite for any fixed σ∗>0 (finitely many slabs intersect K) but its worst-case bound from the slab recurrence grows exponentially as O((q−1)diam(K)/d). (Conjecture / unverified expectation: whether generic shift configurations with q≥3 admit another smooth inverse H with polynomial Ck-norm growth as σ∗→0 is not proved here; approximation does not need it. The unshifted rescaling H(u)=(∑j=1q​γj​)−1G^(u) has ∑j​γj​H(u+sj​)−G^(u)=(∑j​γj​)−1∑j​γj​(G^(u+sj​)−G^(u)), and with ∣sj​∣≤Cσ∗ and G^ C2 on a neighbourhood of K, uniform continuity of D2G^ makes this o(1) in C2(K) as σ∗→0; that suffices for η-approximation, bypassing exact shift deconvolution. A rate needs more: O(σ∗) if G^∈C3, and O((σ∗)2) only if also the shifts are centred, ∑j​γj​sj​=0, and G^∈C4. Without centring the first-order term remains: shifts σ,2σ,3σ with equal weights and G^(u)=u2 give error 4σu+314​σ2.)

4. The theorem

Theorem 4 (universal approximation of answers). Fix the nodes, λ, and the predictor’s time features φ(t), with φ(t∗)=φ(tk​) for the node levels tk​=t∗ of some level t∗. Let the network be Eθ​(u,t)=Nθ​(u,φ(t)) (resp. εθ​(u,t)=Nθ​(u,φ(t))) with Nθ​ a one-hidden-layer network whose activation is C2 (resp. C1) and not a polynomial. Let finitely many queries (polarities and precisions), each with compact data Q and window Y, have regular target residuals rΨ​ for a target energy Ψ∈C2 (resp. rG​ for a target field G∈C1). Then for every η>0 there are parameters θ such that, for every one of these queries and every p∈Q, the answers of the learner in Y and of the target in Y are in a bijection that moves no answer by more than η and preserves stability. In particular the learner’s stable answers are within Hausdorff distance η of the target’s, uniformly in p.

Proof. Take K compact containing every z=(zC​,y) with (p,y) in the neighbourhoods W of Lemma 1, for all the queries, and take the smallest δ0​,μ among them. Proposition 3 gives E∗ with UE∗​=Ψ on K, and it can be written as E~(u,φ(t)) with E~ C2 on Rn+f (a smooth factor in the feature vector that is 1 at φ(t∗) and 0 at the other levels’ features). By Pinkus’ theorem, one-hidden-layer networks with a Cm activation that is not a polynomial are dense in Cm on compact sets (with all derivatives up to order m). So for every β>0 some Nθ​ is within β of E~ in C2 on the compact set ⋃k​{(αk​z+σk​εk​,φ(tk​)):z∈K}. Since αk​≤1, the chain rule gives ∥Uθ​−Ψ∥C2(K)​≤β∑k​ck​. The residuals differ by ∇F​Uθ​−∇F​Ψ (the precision term is the same), so their C1 distance in y is at most β∑k​ck​. Choose β∑k​ck​≤min{δ0​,μη/2} and apply Lemma 1. For fields, use C1 and ∑k​wk​λk​. □

Remarks.

  • Time features. The package’s sinusoidal features include the frequency 1/2, (sinπt,cosπt), which is injective on [0,1]. So every level can be singled out.
  • Depth. A deeper network with the same activation can emulate the shallow one: with ς′(a)=0, (ς(a+hx)−ς(a))/(hς′(a))→x in Ck on compact sets as h→0, so the extra layers approximate the identity. swish, tanh and softplus qualify. ReLU is not C1 and is excluded, as it is for energy networks anyway.
  • Noise blocks. With held block levels the node inputs are α⊙z+σ⊙ε with per-coordinate factors, and the weights change (implicit §9). The proof only uses that each node input is an invertible affine map of z and that the weights are positive, so it carries over.
  • ε-networks are universal for a larger class: any field with regular answers, gradient or not. Energy networks reach exactly the gradient targets. Both reach every relation that has an energy (§5).

5. Which relations are regular targets

The theorem is about targets with regular answers. Which relations have one?

  1. Functions. For y=f(x), f∈C2 on a compact D, take Ψ=21​∣y−f(x)∣2 and the query x→y: r=y−f(x), J=I, μ=1. With Y a ball containing f(D) in its interior, the learner’s single answer approximates f uniformly on D. Universal approximation of functions is the special case of one branch. Since Theorem 4 makes Uθ​ close to Ψ in C2, mixed derivatives included, the implicit function theorem also makes the derivative of the answer, ∂x​y, close to f′.
  2. Finitely many branches with dy​=1. For disjoint branches f1​<⋯<fq​ over D, take Ψ=21​∏i​(y−fi​(x))2. The fibre derivative is a polynomial of degree 2q−1 with 2q−1 simple real roots: the minima fi​ (with Ψyy​=∏j=i​(fi​−fj​)2>0) and one maximum between each pair (Rolle). So (R1) holds, and (R2) holds for Y=[−R,R] with R large.
  3. The circle away from its branch points. Ψ=41​(x2+y2−1)2, query x→y, D=[−1+κ,1−κ]. The answers y=±1−x2​ are stable with Ψyy​=2y2≥2κ(2−κ), and y=0 is unstable with Ψyy​=x2−1<0. Then μ∼κ, so the guarantee 2δ/μ degrades as the query approaches x=±1. This is T3 made quantitative. The same Ψ serves the query y→x.
  4. Chains and products. A product of learners, one per factor, reaches every target of the factorised form ∑k​Ψk​(xk−1​,xk​)−∑k​ΨS,k​(xk​). Apply Theorem 4 to each factor; the errors add. examples/chain.jl shows such chains of three to five relations (product §4).

6. What the theorem does not say

explicit (Weierstraß, networks)algebraic (Nash–Tognoli)implicit diffusion (Theorem 4)
approximated objectcontinuous functionscompact smooth manifolds, up to diffeomorphismthe answers of any regular target, per query
in the data’s own space?yesnot necessarily (Borel–Haefliger)yes
effective?yes (rates)nothe persistence step yes (2δ/μ); the network step as effective as C2 rates for networks
nondegenerate output?n/asingular fits are genericanswers within δ0​ of a regular target are regular

Gap 2 of Nash–Tognoli does not arise here: everything stays in the data’s coordinates, and smooth functions carry no algebraic-cycle obstruction. Gap 3 (singular fits) is answered near a regular target, because Lemma 1 makes the learner’s answers regular there as well. What remains open:

  1. Expressiveness is not learning. Training does not aim at Ψ. At its population optimum the network is the true score, E∗(⋅,t)=−logpt​. The learner’s relation is then the set of stable answers of the smoothed density’s U∗, the ridge of Implicit Diffusion Learners §5. That is not the data’s relation: the smoothing bias (T2) adds up along chains (examples/chain.jl). Theorem 4 does say something here. If U∗ is regular and the trained network is C2-close to −logpt​ on the query region, the answers are those of U∗ within 2δ/μ. But score matching controls an L2(pt​) error, not a C2 error on the query region, and the two differ exactly where data are sparse (T5). Consistency is open: that the answers of U∗ approach the data’s relation as the levels go to 0 and the data grow. Related results exist for ridges of kernel density estimates (Genovese et al. 2014).
  2. Rates. Lemma 1 is effective, and the network step inherits whatever C2 approximation rates hold for E~. Those rates depend on its smoothness and on the input dimension n+f. The construction of Lemma 2 is not frugal: with antithetic shifts ±s it sums about diam(K)/∣s∣ translates. In practice the natural target −logpt​ is smooth at the node levels and needs no deconvolution. Rates for the realistic target are open.
  3. Branch points (T3). (R1) excludes them, and the constant degrades like 1/κ near them. Folds of the answer set are generic in families of functions and structurally stable, so a statement that the fold itself persists and moves by O(δ) should hold. It is not proved here.
  4. Which relations are regular targets. §5 proves it for functions, for scalar multi-branch relations and for the circle. For a general compact submanifold M⊂Rn and every polarity at once, it is open. Ψ=21​∣ϕ∣2 with a defining submersion ϕ makes the answers on M regular exactly where M is a local graph over the clamped coordinates. But it needs a trivial normal bundle, and it does not control the other critical points of the fibres. This is the implicit counterpart of the question Nash–Tognoli answers for varieties.
  5. Finding the answers (T1, T11). The theorem gives existence and the count of answers per query, not that the solver finds all of them, or any of them, from a given start.
  6. Queries outside Q (T4, T5). Nothing is claimed off the compact query set. The learner may have answers there that the target lacks.

7. Multi-polarity queries and the codimension paradox (heuristic analysis and conjecture)

Status: heuristic analysis and conjecture (not proved). The three regimes below describe the expected geometry of the fibre Hessian JFF​ across query polarities of different dimensions; making regime 2 rigorous as a theorem requires nondegeneracy assumptions on the smoothed in-manifold log-density as σt​→0 that remain open (see §6.1 and §6.4).

For any choice of free coordinates F⊆{1,…,n} with m=∣F∣, the fibre residual rθ​(p,y)=∇zF​​Uθ​(zC​,y)+ρF2​⊙(y−z0,F​)=0 is a square m×m system of equations in m unknowns. How can a single scalar potential Uθ​:Rn→R yield isolated, regular answers (symJFF​≻0) across queries with different numbers of free coordinates m (for instance, MNIST classification image -> label with m=10 alongside conditional generation label -> image with m=784)?

Suppose the data lie on a smooth submanifold M⊂Rn of dimension dM​=n−k and codimension k. Three distinct geometric regimes govern the fibre Hessian JFF​=(∇2Uθ​)FF​+diag(ρF2​):

  1. Transverse manifold slicing (m=k, ρF​=0). If Uθ​(z)=21​dist(z,M)2 is a pure tubular distance potential vanishing on M, then at every z∈M the full Hessian ∇2Uθ​(z) has rank k (positive definite on the normal space Nz​M) and corank dM​=n−k (zero along the tangent space Tz​M). When m=k and the m-dimensional coordinate fibre RF intersects M transversely (Tz​M⊕RF=Rn), the restriction JFF​ is strictly positive definite (rankJFF​=k=m), so the intersection points are isolated regular answers.
  2. Conditional mode finding (m>k, ρF​=0). If Uθ​ were constant along M and m>k, the intersection M∩({zC​}×RF) would be an (m−k)-dimensional continuum of roots and JFF​ would have corank at least m−k≥1 everywhere on M, violating (R1). A diffusion learner’s potential U∗(z)≈−logpt​(z), however, is not constant along M: it carries the negative log-density of the data distribution on M alongside the normal penalty 2σt2​1​dist(z,M)2. When m>k, the roots of ∇zF​​U∗(zC​,y)=0 are the 0-dimensional local modes of the conditional density on the slice M∩({zC​}×RF), where the in-manifold density curvature breaks the tangent degeneracy and makes JFF​≻0.
  3. Soft-anchored projection (m>k, ρF​>0). When the free coordinates carry positive anchor precisions ρF​>0, adding diag(ρF2​)≻0 makes JFF​=(∇2Uθ​)FF​+diag(ρF2​)≻0 even when Uθ​ is flat along M, selecting the metric projection of the anchor z0,F​ onto the slice.
