Training Energy-Based Models

05 Nov 202613 min read

  • annotation
  • definition

annotation definition

Energy-Based Learning §3 says Lenticulum has energies and no loss functionals. This note is the modern answer to what a loss functional should be: two of the three standard answers are implemented in this repository, under other names, and the third (maximum likelihood with MCMC) is the one Lenticulum never needed for training. It then lists the newer options that avoid MCMC, and which of them would add something here (§4), notes how score matching relates to the Ritz method for PDEs (§5), and says what is still missing (§6).

Updated 2026-11-05: §2.1 now records EnergyNetwork (an energy-parametrised score, implemented since this note was first written); §3’s “Langevin is missing” is out of date (implicit_sample’s correctors and SMC); §4 and §5 are new; §6 replaces the old list of gaps, three of which had been closed.

1. The problem

An EBM defines pθ​(x)∝e−Eθ​(x) with Zθ​ unknown. Maximum likelihood needs

∇θ​logpθ​(x)=−∇θ​Eθ​(x)+Ex′∼pθ​​[∇θ​Eθ​(x′)]

— push down on the data, push up on the model’s own samples. That second term is Energy-Based Learning §3’s contrastive term, made concrete: it is an expectation under the model, and sampling from the model is the whole difficulty.

Song & Kingma organise the escape routes into three.

The middle two rows are the finding. The MCMC row’s cost, a chain per gradient step, is the one Lenticulum never pays in training.

2.1 Score matching is the diffusion package

∇x​logpθ​(x)=−∇x​Eθ​(x), and the gradient is taken in x, so Zθ​ — a constant in x — differentiates away. That is why score matching needs no normaliser and no sampling.

Denoising score matching is the diffusion objective. VariationalDiffusion’s predictor.md §2 records the identity

sθ​(x,t)=−σt​εθ​(x,t)​≈∇x​logpt​(x)

which is exactly Song & Kingma’s score-matching estimator with the noise scale as the smoothing parameter. So the package built for The Diffusion Family is, without ever saying so, an energy-based model trained by score matching — and ImplicitREDDiff’s regulariser is that trained energy being reused at inference time.

The connection is not decorative. It explains a fact reddiff.md §4 found empirically and could not account for: the calibration constant λ⋆, derived there so that RED-Diff’s implied prior matches a Gaussian’s, is doing the job the missing normaliser would have done. A score model knows ∇x​logp and not logp; the constant of integration is exactly what λ is standing in for, and that is why one scalar cannot fit a correlated prior (RED-Diff as a Statistical Game).

The energy-parametrised score (Salimans & Ho 2021; Du et al. 2023) closes most of that gap, and it is implemented: EnergyNetwork outputs a scalar Eθ​(x,t) and the noise prediction is σt​∇x​Eθ​ (energy_network). It trains by the same denoising loss, with no MCMC; the price is a second derivative, about three times the time per step on the circle (41 s against 15 s, energy_network §6). In return the field is exactly a gradient, the implicit learner has an energy U(z), and two answers to one query can be compared. What it still lacks is the absolute level: Eθ​(⋅,t) is −logpt​ only up to a constant in x, which may differ per noise level and per model (§6).

2.2 NCE is the ratio factor — and it recovers the normaliser

Noise-contrastive estimation trains a classifier to separate data from a known noise distribution q. Adversarial’s ratio.md §1 has the identity already:

logitD∗(x)=logp(x)−logq(x)

In a GAN, q is the generator and is unknown, so you get a ratio and stop. In NCE, q is chosen and known, so

logp(x)=logitD∗(x)+logq(x)

and you have the normalised log-density, partition function included. That is the whole trick, and it changes what RatioFactor is worth:

RatioFactor with a known noise distribution is belief_logdensity

RatioFactor now emits a LogPotentialBelief for logr(x), and Mycelium can pool it with explicit particles by importance weighting (ratio, LP-016). With known logq(x), adding it to the log ratio gives a density potential for p rather than the ratio potential p/q. These are different messages: their products with a cavity have different meanings.

Two particle clouds still have no product rule without a density or potential. RatioFactor supplies scoring and explicit posterior reweighting; it does not train the classifier. The identity above requires the optimal equal-prior discriminator.

3. Du & Mordatch: composition is the factor graph

Du & Mordatch scale EBM training with Langevin dynamics plus a replay buffer, and report three capabilities. Two of them are things this project gets structurally rather than as a technique.

Compositionality. Independently trained EBMs compose by adding energies — Eand​=E1​+E2​ — giving concept conjunction with no retraining. That is presented as a notable property of EBMs.

It is what a factor graph is. E=∑c​Ec​ is Energy-Based Factor Graphs §1, it is GradedEnergy’s ⊕, and it is Mycelium.combine in the log domain. Lenticulum does not have to discover compositionality; the whole architecture is that operation.

The difference is what happens next: Du & Mordatch compose energies and then sample by Langevin; Lenticulum composes energies and then passes messages. Same composition, two inference algorithms — and for Dirac-valued factors Lenticulum’s is min-sum, which is the T→0 limit of the Gibbs distribution Langevin is sampling.

Inpainting and corrupt-image reconstruction. Clamp some coordinates, minimise over the rest. That is ImplicitREDDiff’s selection matrices Pin​+Pout​+Platent​=Id and DiffusionFactor’s precision_vector — The Diffusion Factor. Again: a capability there, a polarity here.

Langevin as an inference mode. When this note was first written, sampling from a composed energy was the one thing Du & Mordatch had that this project did not. It exists now: implicit_sample samples a relation or a product of relations by the reverse diffusion, with Langevin or MALA corrector steps at every noise level and optionally SMC weights (sampler, product §5); a product needs the correctors, because the sum of the factors’ scores is not the score of the noised product. RED-Diff’s prox is still gradient descent on an energy, MAP rather than a sample: the difference between T=0 and T=1 in Energy-Based Factor Graphs §3.

4. Newer options without MCMC, and which would add something here

Since Song & Kingma’s survey the field has moved in three directions: MCMC made cheap (conditional or warm-started chains), contrastive losses with better negatives, and energies learned with diffusion or flow objectives. Two of these are the most promising here. Energy discrepancy gives a loss to the energy factors that today have only collapse warnings. Energy and Equilibrium Matching would replace the implicit learner’s sum over noise levels by one time-independent field, whose roots are the relation and whose descent is the sampler. Both are listed in Feature Backlog.

5. Score matching is a Ritz method

The Ritz method solves a PDE by minimising its energy functional over a family of functions. The Deep Ritz method (E & Yu 2018) takes the family to be a network and estimates the integral by Monte Carlo over sample points: for −Δu=f,

I[u]=∫(21​∥∇u∥2−fu)dx.

Hyvärinen’s score matching has the same shape. The Fisher divergence 21​Ep​∥∇x​Eθ​+∇x​logp∥2 contains the unknown ∇x​logp; integration by parts moves the derivative onto Eθ​ and leaves, up to a constant,

J(θ)=Ex∼p​[21​∥∇x​Eθ​(x)∥2−Δx​Eθ​(x)].

In both, a functional quadratic in a gradient is estimated by sampling, after the integration by parts that a weak (variational) form needs; the data samples play the role of the quadrature points. This is an analogy of form, not a theorem of this vault. It explains the cost: Δx​Eθ​, a Hessian trace, is the expensive term, and sliced score matching (random projections) and denoising score matching (a noise level instead of the trace) are the two standard ways to avoid it. The diffusion objective used here is the second.

6. What is still missing

  1. A loss for a general energy factor. Constraint and Gaussian factors are normalised in their data channels, diffusion factors train by the denoising loss, and every other learned energy (toric factors with normalised = false, a DEQ with its state observed) is at least warned about (Why Learned Relations Do Not Collapse). Nothing trains such a factor correctly: that needs a contrastive term. Energy discrepancy (§4) or NCE with a good noise would be that term.
  2. The absolute level of a learned energy. logpartition still exists only for GaussianBelief, and an EnergyNetwork’s energy is −logpt​ up to a constant per level and per model. That is enough for gradients, roots, MALA and comparing two answers to one query. It is not enough to compare models, structures or noise levels by their energies, or to make scalar_free_energy a log-evidence beyond the Gaussian case. NCE against a known noise, or a ratio estimate through intermediate distributions, would supply the constant.
  3. A time-independent energy. The implicit learner sums over noise levels; one level-free field (§4, Energy and Equilibrium Matching) is untried.
