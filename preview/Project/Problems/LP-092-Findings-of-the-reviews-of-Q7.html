LP-092 Findings of the reviews of Q7

26 Nov 20269 min read

problem

Status: closed (2026-11-25). Q7 (device-generic factors; Codex) and what its two reviews found. Eight defects were reproduced by a failing test and fixed, each test kept in lib/LenticulumCore.jl/test/device_arrays.jl or lib/Lenticulum.jl/test/lp092.jl. Three came from Mistral’s probes and five from Gemini’s reading. All of them sat on shapes or configurations that Q4’s one-dimensional fixtures do not build: wrapped device matrices, dimension 2, a default finite-difference Jacobian, a predictor that fixes the latent. Gemini also listed device defects on the message paths that Q7’s row does not cover: mean-field, plates, factor beliefs, EP and mixture reduction, clusters. Two were reproduced; the rest are claims. They are row Q20. The benchmark against dev-danielb then found the CPU path up to 1.65× slower in four places, from lost inference; fixed, with an inference test. Related: Engineering Standard, Backlog Q Quality and Engineering (Q7, Q8, Q19, Q20), Feature Backlog.

Sources: Codex (GPT-6.2, codex exec -s workspace-write, medium effort; brief in scratch/q7/brief.md of ../Lenticulum.jl-codex; it stopped at its 5-hour usage limit with the implementation done and most gates run, but without its final report, so this note is written from its logs, scratch/q7/). Mistral Large (vibe --agent review, $0, 100 turns, no answer file: many of its commands were refused in the headless run; its probes in scratch/q7r/ of ../Lenticulum.jl-mistral, which the session ran). What Q7 does

Every factor type’s messages, energy, local_free_energy and M-step run on a device array type with scalar indexing disallowed, and return arrays of the input’s array type. The design, written before Codex started: dense SPD algebra on GPU arrays is written in broadcasts and views (a right-looking Cholesky, triangular substitution, inverse, log-determinant); eigen and SVD rank decisions go through one helper, _host_spectral, which copies the small matrix to the host and returns the results on the input’s array type; there is no @allowscalar in lib/*/src; Adapt.jl is a hard dependency of LenticulumCore, Mycelium and Lenticulum, and every struct holding numeric arrays has adapt_structure, the factor graph among them; the CPU path is unchanged. Before Q7, LenticulumCore refused dense device Gaussians on purpose (“Hardware dense Gaussian algebra is not claimed”, gaussian_belief.md); Q7 reverses that.

The test (device_matrix.jl) runs each package’s element-type fixtures, and Lenticulum’s M-step fixtures, on Array, views, SArray and JLArray (Float64 and Float32). It checks values against the Array call and, on JLArray, that every numeric array of the result is a JLArray. Lenticulum 458 checks in the default tier (2 min 55 s alone), AlgebraicFactors 68, LenticulumCore 112. device_graphs.jl adapts a two-dimensional Kalman chain and a switching model to JLArray, runs inference and compares beliefs and free energy with the CPU run.

One deviation from the design: a scalar needed as control or metadata is read through _entry(A, i) (a one-element reduction), at 57 call sites. The design asked for one read of a reduced value. Each _entry is a device synchronisation, so loops over regimes or quadrature nodes pay one per term. This is correct on every backend and not measured; Q8 measures it on hardware.

CPU results are bitwise unchanged: Codex’s record of Q4’s 99 fixture operations (scratch/q7/record.jl), rerun by the session after all fixes (99/99), and the session’s record of the 102 operations of the wide fixtures against dev-danielb at 7e6a6659 (identical).

Q20 follow-up on device paths

The extended JLArray matrix in lib/Lenticulum.jl/test/device_inference.jl reproduced failures in backward Gaussian gradient messages, expanded mixtures, switching and clustered plates, frozen learned-prior beliefs, and the noisy hard constraint’s free energy. The matrix passed after the storage fixes. The adapted graph test in lib/Lenticulum.jl/test/device_graphs.jl also reproduced scalar access during plate convergence and now checks raw blocks, marginal beliefs and free energy.

The targeted tests in lib/Lenticulum.jl/test/device_paths.jl reproduced mixture pruning, Sampson point localisation, categorical score sampling, scale-shift initialisation, and constructor storage defects. Mixture log density, EP projection, EP division, and flat coordinate pinning already matched the CPU in their JLArray probes. The mixture log-density broadcast now aligns storage, and EP avoids per-component device reads and the host precision check. The diagonal Gaussian score replay matches the CPU. The dense replay remains a broken test because reverse AD reaches the dense device Cholesky. On CUDA and on JLArrays 0.5 seven more host paths showed, fixed in LP-095.

The benchmark: four CPU slowdowns (session)

benchmark/compare.jl --base dev-danielb --packages LenticulumCore,Mycelium,MyceliumLearning,Lenticulum --interleave (one thread, two interleaved runs each) on the reviewed and fixed tree flagged Lenticulum/message/nonlinear at 1.65× and Mycelium/grid/flooding_sweep at 1.61× (with 55 % more allocations), although the CPU results were bitwise unchanged.
