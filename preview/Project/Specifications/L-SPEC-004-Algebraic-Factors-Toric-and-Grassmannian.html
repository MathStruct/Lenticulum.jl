design

Status: partly implemented (2026-10-26): §0, §1.1–§1.7, §2.1–§2.3, §3(a,b), §4 and §5; §2.4 remains planned. Originally planned on Daniel’s request (2026-10-25, “Could you plan for that?”), following Gemini’s suggestion to begin with toric models and the Grassmannian. Related: Toric Models as Factors, Grassmann Mixing and Plücker Coordinates, The Parameter is a Grassmannian, Algebraic Implicit Learners, Open Problems in Algebraic Implicit Learning, L-SPEC-001 The Factor Interface, L-SPEC-003 Exposed Parameters and Energy Channels. Mycelium supplies the public combine_with_overlap rule shared by mixture pooling and homotopy branch weights.

Scope

The first code for the algebraic family (Algebraic Implicit Learners) implements finite toric factors, Grassmannian parameter updates and forward Plücker transport; polynomial residuals, complete-data fitting and single-branch Gaussian messages are implemented. Gemini’s review (Proposal 7 of 05_Architectural_Design_Proposals.md in the review folder) sketches a whole package lib/AlgebraicFactors.jl; this specification orders it so that the cases where everything is exact or cheap come first:

  1. toric factors (Toric Models as Factors): exact discrete factors with known implicit equations;
  2. Grassmannian parameters (The Parameter is a Grassmannian): projection and retraction for parameters defined up to a gauge;
  3. the general polynomial factor, on top of both;
  4. multi-branch inference by homotopy continuation, last.

Plücker coordinates (Grassmann Mixing and Plücker Coordinates) enter as a small deterministic factor and as test data. The contract below is a proposal; each part becomes binding when it is implemented with its tests.

The proposed contract

§0 Package. lib/AlgebraicFactors.jl, depending on LenticulumCore, Mycelium and LinearAlgebra, plus LuxCore and Random for the parameter/state interface, until §4 adds a package extension. Tests and docs are wired like the other packages under lib/.

§1 Toric factors.

  • §1.1 ToricFactor(names::Tuple, alphabets::Tuple, A::AbstractMatrix{<:Integer}; normalised = false): one channel per name, of the alphabet’s size. The joint states are ordered column-major over the alphabets (documented), and column x of A is ax​; the constructor checks size(A, 2) == prod(alphabets). Constructors build A for common cases: independence(names, alphabets) and hierarchical(names, alphabets, generators) (marginal-cell indicators per generator).
  • §1.2 Parameters ps = (η = …,) in Rd. pη​=pη′​ iff A⊤(η−η′)∈span{1} (the docstring says so); an optional reduction to independent logit differences is a chart, not a change of model. Statements that need 1 in the row space of A use Aˉ=(A1⊤​) internally (Toric Models as Factors).
  • §1.3 energy(f, x, …) is −⟨η,ax​⟩, plus Λ(η) when normalised; x is a tuple of labels. Scalar.
  • §1.4 factor_message(f, target, polarity, inputs, prior, ps, st) -> (belief, st): the exact sum-product likelihood message to target, a CategoricalBelief. Observed inputs may be Diracs on labels or categorical beliefs; latent channels (missing or uninformative inputs, which Mycelium resolves to Latent()) are summed over with unit weight, so ordinary BP initialisation works. The message excludes the target’s prior. The cost, ∏ alphabets per message, is stated.
  • §1.5 local_free_energy(f, msgs, ps, st): Ebf​​E−H(bf​) for the factor belief bf​∝ψη​∏u​μu→f​, exact, in the form PriorFactor returns it (a scalar). With normalised = true, the game-level free_energy and entropy of L-SPEC-001 The Factor Interface §4 are exact as well.
  • §1.6 fit_mle(f, counts; method = :newton | :ipf), returning η, a convergence status and, separately, an existence status. The stopping test is moment matching, ∥Ap^​−Au/n∥ (Birch), which can also approach zero at an extended MLE on the boundary. Without an existence certificate (§Open), a diverging η is reported as non-convergence or suspected boundary convergence, never as proven non-existence, and never silently. Newton removes the gauge directions first.
  • §1.7 inmodel(f, p; atol): for positive p, whether logp lies in the row space of Aˉ (least squares). binomial_residuals(f, p, moves) evaluates pu+−pu− for moves the user supplies. No Markov-basis computation.

§2 Grassmannian parameters.

  • §2.1 Grassmann(k, m), represented by Θ∈Rk×m with ΘΘ⊤=Ik​: project_tangent(M, Θ, G) =G−GΘ⊤Θ; retract(M, Θ, Δ) orthonormalises the rows of Θ+Δ by a thin QR of the transpose, with signs chosen so that diag(R)>0, so that RΘ​(0)=Θ (in exact arithmetic; tested with ≈); rand(rng, M) and ispoint(M, Θ; atol). The projection is the horizontal lift for Gr, not the full Stiefel tangent projection. Non-finite and numerically rank-deficient QR inputs throw (threshold documented in grassmann). (Edelman, Arias & Smith 1998; Absil, Mahony & Sepulchre 2008.)
  • §2.2 Retracted(rule, M) <: AbstractUpdateRule for Mycelium’s optimiser lens, at first for GradientDescent and Momentum only, with rule_get(...) = Θ. rule_put projects the gradient, applies rule, converts the candidate point it returns into a displacement p′−Θ, projects that onto the tangent space and retracts. A rule’s state (momentum) is projected onto the new tangent space, which is a vector transport for an embedded manifold (Absil, Mahony & Sepulchre 2008). Mycelium has no rule-state initializer: callers supply nothing for gradient descent or a plain zero(Θ) velocity matrix for momentum. Its recurrence is s′=γs−ηg (corrected in LP-015 Momentum had the wrong sign on its decay), which the wrapper preserves. Nesterov waits: its look-ahead p+γs leaves the manifold, and a retracted look-ahead with a consistent transport of the gradient has to be specified first. This is the lens G∗(Θ,Δ)=qf(Θ+ΠΘ​Δ) of The Parameter is a Grassmannian.
  • §2.3 Gauge invariance as a test obligation (implemented with a test-only residual, not the §3 factor): a row-space relation uses the same Sampson scalar energy for Θ and BΘ when the covariance is numerically invertible. Following L-ADR-0007 Point-dependent scalarisations are localised before use (accepted 2026-10-25), core SampsonNorm is localised at the factor and point to SquaredNorm((JΣJᵀ + λR)⁻¹). Unlocalised application and jensen_gap throw. Default damping is identity; a factor with a gauge overrides sampson_damping with R↦BRB⊤, here R=ΘΘ⊤. This keeps damped energy invariant even at rank-deficient J, provided the covariance passes the SPD check. Numerically singular covariance throws; there is no silent pseudoinverse. Graded scalarisations with point-dependent parts are rejected until per-part factor/argument routing is specified. energy gives the numerical threshold, reference Jacobian and frozen-metric Gauss–Newton convention.
  • §2.4 With L-SPEC-003 Exposed Parameters and Energy Channels, an exposed Grassmannian parameter is a channel whose space is a manifold; that spec’s “keep open” list already allows it.

§3 The polynomial factor. Parts (a) and (b) implemented.

  • ExponentTable(N,d; basis=:monomial|:chebyshev, homogeneous=false) stores N×m indices in increasing total degree, descending lexicographic order. veronese, veronese_jacobian use polynomial arithmetic/three-term recurrences. No data scaling is implicit; Chebyshev is intended for [-1,1] without enforcement. basis_change(mon,cheb) is exact for affine tables; degree-exact Chebyshev products generally leave the homogeneous monomial span, which throws.
  • PolynomialFactor(dims,tab,k;Σ=I,λ=0,iterations=1) has seeded Grassmann rows and empty state. Raw energy accepts named values split between x/y, concatenated in dims order. Sampson energy/Jacobian uses x=nothing, packed y=z, wrt=:y, all coordinates. sampson_damping=ΘΘ′; λ≥0 preserves row-gauge invariance. This coefficient metric does not survive nonorthogonal polynomial basis changes; undamped energy does. One single-target polarity per channel is declared; rank is computed at message time. See polynomial.
  • fit_nullspace(tab,X,k;rtol=1e-12) uses the bottom right singular vectors, returns Θ, the full spectrum (structural zeros included), numerical_rank, nullity and threshold; there is no column scaling or normalisation.
  • fit_sampson(tab,X,k;Σ=I,Θ0=nothing,method=RiemannianTrustRegion(),tol=1e-7, maxiter=10000,λ=0) starts at the nullspace fit by default. Full analytic gradient, horizontal projection, QR retraction and exchangeable methods minimise the non-convex summed half squared Sampson objective. The stopping rule is horizontal Frobenius gradient norm ≤tol; results report cost, gradient_norm, converged and iterations (RTR counts rejected outer trials too, and adds hessian_products). RiemannianGradient exposes Armijo tunables; RiemannianTrustRegion uses analytic Hessian-vector products with Grassmann curvature correction and Steihaug–Toint CG. Symbols :riemannian, :fns, :trust_region alias defaults. A user AbstractSampsonMethod implements the documented sampson_solve hook with SampsonProblem objective/geometry. Singular current/trial covariances throw with point index; optional λ adds gauge-compatible damping. method=:fns is the published corrected iteration for k=1 (throws otherwise), with the same gradient stopping criterion. Naive frozen-weight IRLS is not a substitute for this objective’s gradient. See polyfit for derivation, verified citation, numerical checks and limitations. No global optimum claimed.
  • Part (b): single-branch Gaussian sites use exact J and A=JΣ_fJᵀ+JₒCᵢₙJₒᵀ+λΘΘᵀ, including all factor data coordinates. Λ=JᵤᵀA⁻¹Jᵤ, h=Λx₀−JᵤᵀA⁻¹r₀. Observed beliefs are Dirac/proper Gaussian; others throw. The target starts at the proper Gaussian/Dirac cavity mean, or zero without information. iterations≥1 repeats posterior linearisation, retaining the original cavity and freezing A per step. No safeguard or general descent guarantee; this is Gauss–Newton curvature, not the full Laplace Hessian away from zero residual, nor a minimiser of the full varying-metric Sampson cost. Numerical rank Jᵤ is computed at every point; deficient precision is retained as an improper site (flat on ker Jᵤ), including rank zero. Without a unique posterior mode further iteration stops. k>q is weighted least squares. Singular A throws; positive λ may rescue it with full-row-rank Θ, never a pseudoinverse. likelihood_message/factor_message share the site routine; invert combines with the prior once. The default does not enumerate branches; §4 adds the optional mixture. No IFT rules or game/free-energy/entropy implementation (LP-009 Game-level free energy is declared but not implemented). See Inference as Root Finding and polynomial.

§4 Multi-branch inference (implemented, optional): a HomotopyContinuation.jl package extension (AlgebraicFactorsHomotopyContinuationExt, a weak dependency) for PolynomialFactor, returning a MixtureBelief over branches (Branches and the Discriminant). Gemini’s Proposal 7 item 5 sketches it with weights wj​∝π(xj​)/∣detJu​(xj​)∣ and an “exact” entropy −∑j​wj​logwj​; both need the qualifications below, which are part of the contract.

  • §4.1 Square systems only (k=q for the target). Nonempty regular fibres for k<q are positive-dimensional sets (witness sets, and weights with detJu​Ju⊤​​ on the fibre); for k>q there are generically no roots. Both throw, naming the alternative (§3’s single-branch site). Even square systems can have positive-dimensional zero sets; this API requires isolated roots.
  • §4.2 Homotopy continuation returns complex isolated solutions: keep the real ones by a stated imaginary-part tolerance, and report each root’s certification status (HC’s certify, interval arithmetic/Krawczyk certification) without claiming more. Singular roots (detJu​=0, on the discriminant), solutions at infinity and failed paths are reported, not silently dropped or weighted.
  • §4.3 Weights: for the linearised branch j with residual covariance Aj​ (as in §3(b)) and a Gaussian prior N(μ,P), ∫N(Jj​(x−xj​);0,Aj​)N(x;μ,P)dx=N(xj​;μ,P+Jj−1​Aj​Jj−⊤​)/∣detJj​∣, which tends to Gemini’s π(xj​)/∣detJj​∣ as Aj​→0. Use the former, test both. Components are the branch posteriors (prior times branch site); the returned message excludes the prior, as everywhere else, so the likelihood message is a mixture of normalised branch sites with masses proportional to 1/∣detJj​∣; combining with the prior supplies the Gaussian overlap and reproduces posterior invert. Using posterior masses in the message would apply that overlap twice. A trivial prior means constant density in target Lebesgue measure and gives masses proportional to 1/∣detJj​∣. The exact soft posterior exp(−r2/(2A(x)))π(x) has this small-noise limit only for branch-independent A (otherwise it needs the residual-density normaliser); circle/cubic quadrature tests use constant A.
  • §4.4 Entropy: −∑j​wj​logwj​ is only the entropy of the branch label. For a Gaussian mixture ∑j​wj​Hj​≤H≤∑j​wj​Hj​+H(w); report these bounds and H(w), not an exact H.
  • §4.5 No regular real root: inference throws and directs callers to inspection, which returns diagnostics and nothing mixtures, never an empty mixture. Singular roots are unweighted, including when they are the only real roots.
  • Later: parameter homotopy across calls (tracking last step’s roots as Θ or xo​ moves), which is the cheap path in training loops. Not in this step.

§5 Plücker (implemented). plucker_pairs(r) lists increasing pairs in lexicographic order. plucker(z,w) evaluates pij​=zi​wj​−zj​wi​; plucker_jacobian(z,w) has columns in vcat(z,w) order. plucker_relations(r) returns (4r​) records in lexicographic quadruple order, each with indices=(i,j,k,l), three monomials (pairs of indices into p) and coefficients=(1,-1,1), representing pij​pkl​−pik​pjl​+pil​pjk​. plucker_residuals(r,p) evaluates them.

PluckerFactor(r; names=(:z,:w,:p), covariance_jitter=0) has empty parameters/state, and exactly the forward polarity (observed z,w, unobserved p). The inverse of a nonzero decomposable bivector is an SL2​-orbit of pairs, not a built belief; p=0 is degenerate. Dirac pairs give a Dirac; two sample clouds are paired by index, with equal positive counts and matching normalised weights (rtol=1e-12, atol=0). The first cloud’s weights survive; a Dirac may accompany either cloud. Separate proper Gaussian inputs are assumed independent; the output GaussianBelief matches the exact first two moments, not the generally non-Gaussian law. plucker_moments(μ,S,ν,T) exposes those moments with zero covariance for a Dirac. plucker derives the full bilinear covariance, including the term absent from Jacobian-only propagation. Core Gaussian beliefs cannot represent singular covariance: by default a covariance with λmin​≤neps(T)λmax​ is rejected; explicit positive covariance_jitter adds that absolute variance to each output coordinate, an approximation. Exact singular moments remain available without jitter through plucker_moments. No core or Mycelium changes were needed.

Scalar pointwise energy and graph-local free energy are zero, following GeneratorFactor. Game-level quantities and an assembled lens remain absent (LP-009); the full common conformance checker requires game quantities and therefore does not apply. The 400-point Klein-quadric check now exercises §3(a) fit_nullspace using this map and plucker_relations as its independent oracle.

Open

  • General MLE existence certification: the implemented sufficient certificate is all-positive counts. Deciding relative-interior membership of (1,Au/n) in the convex hull of the columns of Aˉ (Fienberg & Rinaldo 2012) can be formulated as a linear program; whether to depend on an LP solver. The boundary diagnostic is only a heuristic.
  • Markov bases need external software (4ti2 and similar); whether to depend on it.
  • Large tables: messages are exponential in arity. Whether hierarchical should split into one factor per generator automatically is a junction-tree question (LP-010 Where the graph of a problem comes from).
  • Beliefs on the Grassmannian (rather than points on it) are not planned.
