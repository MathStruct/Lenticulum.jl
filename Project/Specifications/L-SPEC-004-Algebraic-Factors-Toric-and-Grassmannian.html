L-SPEC-004 Algebraic Factors Toric and Grassmannian

25 Oct 20268 min read

  • design

design

Status: planned (2026-10-25, Daniel: “Could you plan for that?”, on Gemini’s suggestion to start the algebraic family with the easier and useful cases, toric varieties and the Grassmannian). Related: Toric Models as Factors, Grassmann Mixing and Plücker Coordinates, The Parameter is a Grassmannian, Algebraic Implicit Learners, Open Problems in Algebraic Implicit Learning, L-SPEC-001 The Factor Interface, L-SPEC-003 Exposed Parameters and Energy Channels. Code: none yet; it would touch lib/Mycelium.jl/src/factors.jl (update rules), messages.jl (categorical beliefs), free_energy.jl.

Scope

The first code for the algebraic family, whose sixteen notes (Algebraic Implicit Learners) have no implementation. Gemini’s review (Proposal 7 of 05_Architectural_Design_Proposals.md in the review folder) sketches a whole package lib/AlgebraicFactors.jl; this specification orders it so that the cases where everything is exact or cheap come first:

  1. toric factors (Toric Models as Factors): exact discrete factors with known implicit equations;
  2. Grassmannian parameters (The Parameter is a Grassmannian): projection and retraction for parameters defined up to a gauge;
  3. the general polynomial factor, on top of both;
  4. multi-branch inference by homotopy continuation, last.

Plücker coordinates (Grassmann Mixing and Plücker Coordinates) enter as a small deterministic factor and as test data. The contract below is a proposal; each part becomes binding when it is implemented with its tests.

The proposed contract

§0 Package. lib/AlgebraicFactors.jl, depending on LenticulumCore, Mycelium and LinearAlgebra only, until §4 adds a package extension. Tests and docs are wired like the other packages under lib/.

§1 Toric factors.

  • §1.1 ToricFactor(names::Tuple, alphabets::Tuple, A::AbstractMatrix{<:Integer}; normalised = false): one channel per name, of the alphabet’s size. The joint states are ordered column-major over the alphabets (documented), and column x of A is ax​; the constructor checks size(A, 2) == prod(alphabets). Constructors build A for common cases: independence(names, alphabets) and hierarchical(names, alphabets, generators) (marginal-cell indicators per generator).
  • §1.2 Parameters ps = (η = …,) in Rd. pη​=pη′​ iff A⊤(η−η′)∈span{1} (the docstring says so); an optional reduction to independent logit differences is a chart, not a change of model. Statements that need 1 in the row space of A use Aˉ=(A1⊤​) internally (Toric Models as Factors).
  • §1.3 energy(f, x, …) is −⟨η,ax​⟩, plus Λ(η) when normalised; x is a tuple of labels. Scalar.
  • §1.4 factor_message(f, target, polarity, inputs, prior, ps, st) -> (belief, st): the exact sum-product likelihood message to target, a CategoricalBelief. Observed inputs may be Diracs on labels or categorical beliefs; latent channels (missing or uninformative inputs, which Mycelium resolves to Latent()) are summed over with unit weight, so ordinary BP initialisation works. The message excludes the target’s prior. The cost, ∏ alphabets per message, is stated.
  • §1.5 local_free_energy(f, msgs, ps, st): Ebf​​E−H(bf​) for the factor belief bf​∝ψη​∏u​μu→f​, exact, in the form PriorFactor returns it (a scalar). With normalised = true, the game-level free_energy and entropy of L-SPEC-001 The Factor Interface §4 are exact as well.
  • §1.6 fit_mle(f, counts; method = :newton | :ipf), returning η, a convergence status and, separately, an existence status. The stopping test is moment matching, ∥Ap^​−Au/n∥ (Birch), which can also approach zero at an extended MLE on the boundary. Without an existence certificate (§Open), a diverging η is reported as non-convergence or suspected boundary convergence, never as proven non-existence, and never silently. Newton removes the gauge directions first.
  • §1.7 inmodel(f, p; atol): for positive p, whether logp lies in the row space of Aˉ (least squares). binomial_residuals(f, p, moves) evaluates pu+−pu− for moves the user supplies. No Markov-basis computation.

§2 Grassmannian parameters.

  • §2.1 Grassmann(k, m), represented by Θ∈Rk×m with ΘΘ⊤=Ik​: project_tangent(M, Θ, G) =G−GΘ⊤Θ; retract(M, Θ, Δ) orthonormalises the rows of Θ+Δ by a thin QR of the transpose, with signs chosen so that diag(R)>0, so that RΘ​(0)=Θ (in exact arithmetic; tested with ≈); rand(rng, M). (Edelman, Arias & Smith 1998; Absil, Mahony & Sepulchre 2008.)
  • §2.2 Retracted(rule, M) <: AbstractUpdateRule for Mycelium’s optimiser lens, at first for GradientDescent and Momentum only, with rule_get(...) = Θ. rule_put projects the gradient, applies rule, converts the candidate point it returns into a displacement p′−Θ, projects that onto the tangent space and retracts. A rule’s state (momentum) is projected onto the new tangent space, which is a vector transport for an embedded manifold (Absil, Mahony & Sepulchre 2008). Nesterov waits: its look-ahead p+γs leaves the manifold, and a retracted look-ahead with a consistent transport of the gradient has to be specified first. This is the lens G∗(Θ,Δ)=qf(Θ+ΠΘ​Δ) of The Parameter is a Grassmannian.
  • §2.3 Gauge invariance as a test obligation: a factor whose relation depends on Θ only through its row space must give the same scalarised energy for Θ and BΘ (B invertible) under its default scalarisation. The Sampson energy 21​r⊤(JJ⊤)−1r with J=Dz​rΘ​ of full row rank has this property (Algebraic versus Geometric Distance), but today’s scalarisations see only the residual, not J; a point- and parameter-dependent scalarisation has to be specified first, with its behaviour at singular points (damping or a pseudoinverse does not keep the invariance by itself). Checked once §3 exists.
  • §2.4 With L-SPEC-003 Exposed Parameters and Energy Channels, an exposed Grassmannian parameter is a channel whose space is a manifold; that spec’s “keep open” list already allows it.

§3 The polynomial factor (after §1 and §2), as in Gemini’s Proposal 7: an exponent table with monomial or Chebyshev basis, the residual rΘ​(z)=Θvd​(z) as the pointwise energy, the Sampson scalarisation (after the interface of §2.3), fit_nullspace and Sampson IRLS (Fitting is a Nullspace Problem), and single-branch messages by Newton with a Laplace Gaussian (Inference as Root Finding).

§4 Multi-branch inference (last): a HomotopyContinuation.jl package extension finding all isolated real roots and returning a MixtureBelief over branches (Branches and the Discriminant).

§5 Plücker. plucker(z, w) and its Jacobian; a deterministic PluckerFactor(r) with the forward polarity only (the inverse message would be a belief on an SL2​-orbit, not built); and plucker_relations(r), the quadrics pij​pkl​−pik​pjl​+pil​pjk​, as test data.

Checked by

Nothing yet. The first tests:

  • §1: the independence model, whose MLE is the outer product of the margins; the chain A−B−C as a decomposable model with positive margins, against p^​AB​p^​BC​/p^​B​; belief propagation on a tree of ToricFactors against enumeration of the joint (marginals and logZ); the gradient Au/n−Apη​ against finite differences; Newton against IPF; inmodel true at a fit and false at a generic p; for a table with an empty row, a status that is not “converged”, and the extended MLE (the outer product of the margins) as the limit; the conformance checker (test/factor_conformance.jl) over every supported polarity, which today calls assemble, invert and likelihood_message: either ToricFactor implements the lens methods, or the checker is extended to direct-message factors with equivalent checks.
  • §2: ΘΘ⊤=I after retract; project_tangent(M, Θ, G) * Θ' ≈ 0; retract(M, Θ, zero(Θ)) ≈ Θ; for symmetric S with λk​<λk+1​, generic initialisation and a suitable step rule, Riemannian descent on tr(ΘSΘ⊤) reaching the span of the k smallest eigenvectors (the nullspace fit’s answer), compared by principal angles and by the objective against ∑i≤k​λi​.
  • §3 with §5: on points z∧w for random z,w∈R4, fit_nullspace of degree 2 should recover the Klein quadric, a one-dimensional null space, with at least 27 generic points and a stated rank tolerance (an expectation, agreeing with a scratch check; Grassmann Mixing and Plücker Coordinates).

Open

  • MLE existence: deciding whether Au/n lies in the relative interior of the convex hull of the columns of Aˉ (Fienberg & Rinaldo 2012) needs a linear program; whether to depend on an LP solver.
  • Markov bases need external software (4ti2 and similar); whether to depend on it.
  • Large tables: messages are exponential in arity. Whether hierarchical should split into one factor per generator automatically is a junction-tree question (LP-010 Where the graph of a problem comes from).
  • Beliefs on the Grassmannian (rather than points on it) are not planned.
  • A causal Grassmann-mixing block (Grassmann Mixing and Plücker Coordinates §“What Lenticulum can take”) is an experiment for LenticulumLM, not part of this package.
